# EduHelpdesk beats free rivals; sign-in and evidence lag

EduHelpdesk already does more than the free tools English school technicians have been abandoning (Spiceworks Cloud, Freshdesk's withdrawn free tier, osTicket), is roughly as broad as the closest UK school product (AssetTap), and covers the core of DfE's new IT support standard: every request records a description, time, requester, priority and actions, staff can follow their own tickets in a portal, and SLAs count school periods and work days. Its real gaps are not ticket handling but **identity, intake and evidence**. Scored on DfE and legal weight (counted double), UK and international demand, and how common a feature is among competitors, the most-wanted addition is **Microsoft 365/Google sign-in with directory sync**: the fourth most frequent UK technician demand, standard in competitors, and the fix for three DfE gaps at once (no MFA for portal users, no password self-reset, no automatic leaver disabling). Second is **email-to-ticket**, the third most common UK want and a channel the IT support standard names, which the owner previously ruled out; the evidence favours revisiting it. Next comes a cluster of DfE evidence features that mostly reuse existing engines (end-of-support and disposal-certificate fields on assets, recurring compliance tasks including the KCSIE filtering-check record, a contracts and licence register, offboarding and approvals), followed by routing rules, a knowledge base and QR stocktakes. AI, MIS sync and US-style parent damage billing rank last. The urgency is real because DfE's filtering, cyber and backup expectations apply "already" or "now", not by 2030, and because no general ITSM vendor maps its features to the DfE standards, which leaves an open niche for a free, self-hosted tool. Two caveats: Reddit was blocked, so practitioner evidence comes from EduGeek and the Spiceworks Community; and the sources disagree on when the IT support standard was published (17 November or 15 December 2025).

## Free, self-hosted and school-shaped: EduHelpdesk outguns the tools schools are leaving

The UK school helpdesk market is thin and led by asset tracking. Only three products found are built for English schools: AssetTap, Civica Education Operations and Every Compliance by IRIS. All three lead with asset registers, loans, audits and trust dashboards rather than deep ticketing ([AssetTap](https://assettap.co.uk/); [Civica](https://www.civica.com/en-us/sector-pages/education/software-multi-academy-trusts/school-operations-and-compliance-software/); [Every G-Cloud](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/631589798045369)). The general ITSM vendors with education pages (HaloITSM, TOPdesk, Freshservice, ManageEngine) back their claims mostly with universities or US districts. US K-12 platforms such as Incident IQ, used by **more than 1,500 districts**, are built around the chain from student to device to guardian, and they price per pupil ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2024/02/incident-iq-receives-strategic-investment-from-cove-hill-partners.html); [Manage1to1 pricing](https://www.manage1to1.com/pricing/compare/)). Many English schools never choose a tool at all: **63% of primaries now get technical support from a managed service provider**, up from 50% in 2023, while 87% of secondaries have in-house support ([DfE Technology in schools survey 2024-25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692834a6ce50d215cae9610e/Technology_in_schools_survey_2024_to_2025_research_report.pdf)). The free tier that small teams relied on has also been dismantled. Spiceworks Cloud started charging every technician seat, at $5–6 per seat per month, for teams of six or more from 1 June 2025 ([Spiceworks announcement](https://community.spiceworks.com/t/spiceworks-cloud-help-desk-announces-platform-enhancements-and-a-new-paid-offering/1187160?print=true); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/spiceworks-ticketing-system)). Freshdesk cut its free plan from ten agents to two in 2024; one school faced a jump to about £6,000 a year for 15 agents, and the remaining free plans were being closed in 2026 ([EduGeek T1](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/219505-helpdesk-options/); [EduGeek T10](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/226668-freshdesk-upcoming-but-undocumented-end-of-free-plan/)). NetSupport ServiceDesk reached end of life on 31 March 2025 ([NetSupport](https://www.netsupportsoftware.com/end-of-life-netsupport-servicedesk)).

That context flatters EduHelpdesk. **Cost is the most frequent theme in recent UK technician threads (9 of them)**. EduHelpdesk is free and MIT-licensed, has no seat cap, and installs as a Windows service with in-place upgrades and a backup first. That answers the main objection to the free self-hosted rivals (osTicket, GLPI, Zammad), which is the pain of setting them up ([feature inventory](../research_notes/School%20helpdesk%20comparison%20and%20priorities/eduhelpdesk_current_features.md); [EduGeek T3](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223340-replacement-for-spiceworks-help-desk/)). Its Spiceworks importer handles repeat imports and can undo the last one, which targets the market's largest documented wave of switching. Spiceworks Cloud has **no SLA tracking and no automatic routing on any plan** ([Siit review](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/spiceworks-review)), and TOPdesk only offers SLAs from its £72-per-agent Engaged tier ([TOPdesk pricing](https://www.topdesk.com/en/pricing/)). EduHelpdesk counts SLAs in lesson periods or school work days and pauses them on chosen statuses, which is more school-aware than the business-hours calendars US tools use. Its search covers comments, asset tags and custom attributes. That matters because poor search is the sharpest complaint against both Incident IQ (a 3/5 G2 review from an assistant technology director in March 2026) and Spiceworks ([G2 Incident IQ](https://www.g2.com/products/incident-iq/reviews); [Spiceworks thread](https://community.spiceworks.com/t/spiceworks-cloud-help-desk-announces-platform-enhancements-and-a-new-paid-offering/1187160?print=true)). Where EduHelpdesk falls behind is the same against all three groups of competitors:

- **Identity:** no Microsoft 365 or Google sign-in.
- **Intake:** no email, no knowledge base, no routing.

## DfE's IT support standard reads like a helpdesk specification

DfE now publishes **12 digital and technology standards**. Six are core, and schools should be working towards them by 2030: broadband, wireless, switching, digital leadership and governance, filtering and monitoring, and cyber security ([GOV.UK collection](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges)). For a helpdesk, the 2030 date is misleading:

- The filtering and monitoring pages say schools should already meet that standard.
- The cyber standard says backups must happen now, and that the user-account expectations should already be met.
- Since 16 September 2026, both pages tie these duties to schools' statutory online-safety responsibility under KCSIE.

Sources: [filtering and monitoring](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard); [cyber security](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard). The Academy Trust Handbook 2026 repeats the 2030 expectation for trusts ([ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)). The standards also change quickly, with **at least ten dated changes between January and September 2026**. Anything EduHelpdesk builds against them should keep its checklists as editable data with a "last checked against DfE" date ([GOV.UK updates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/updates)).

The newest standard, IT support, is not a core standard. The gov.uk change log dates it to 17 November 2025. It is effectively a requirements list for EduHelpdesk. It tells schools to use a "case management or helpdesk system to record and track requests", and sets out these expectations ([IT support standard](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support)):

- **What each request records:** a description, the date and time, the requester, an approver where needed, a priority and the actions taken.
- **Status for staff:** staff can check their request's status and see whether they need to do anything, such as confirm a fix.
- **Response and priority:** schools record how quickly issues will be responded to and resolved, put issues that affect teaching first, and define how serious incidents are escalated.
- **Channels:** several ways to ask for help (it names phone, email and in person), avoiding instant messages.
- **Hours and self-help:** published operating hours, self-service help, and staff resetting their own passwords.
- **Registers:** of assets, software and IT support's own work.
- **Annual review:** the records feed a written review for governors that picks out busy periods and the systems causing most requests.

It sets no numeric targets.

Other duties also fall on the data the helpdesk holds:

- **Cyber standard:** MFA, account lockout, a joiner/mover/leaver process, SLT approval for admin privilege changes, end-of-support dates in the asset register, tested backups, and an internal incident log ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)).
- **KCSIE 2026:** a record of the annual filtering checks ([KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)).
- **UK GDPR:** every breach must be documented ([UK GDPR Art 33](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)).
- **Academy Trust Handbook:** a fixed asset register, and reporting fraud or theft over £5,000 to DfE ([ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)).
- **RPA membership rules:** four cyber conditions that must be evidenced when a claim is made ([RPA rules Sept 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)).

Of these 22 expectations, EduHelpdesk **fully meets 2, mostly meets 3, partly meets 11 and misses 6**. The gaps cluster where DfE is most urgent, in cyber security and filtering, and they concern registers and evidence rather than ticket handling. There is an audience for closing them: only 25% of IT leads who know about the standards say they meet all of them, and 23% of primary IT leads don't know whether their backups meet the guideline ([DfE TiSS 2024-25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692834a6ce50d215cae9610e/Technology_in_schools_survey_2024_to_2025_research_report.pdf)). No general ITSM vendor found maps its features to the standards. AssetTap only says it was built with DfE guidance in mind, and Computeam's Compass tool covers only broadband, filtering and firewall setup ([AssetTap](https://assettap.co.uk/); [Computeam](https://www.computeam.co.uk/videos-and-blog/article/dfes-digital-and-technology-standards-a-comprehensive-overview)).

Cost and ease of running the tool come first, and EduHelpdesk scores well on both. **Email-to-ticket** is the clearest tension with the owner's decisions:

- One poster called Spiceworks Cloud's requirement to email an external Spiceworks address a deal-breaker; another moved to a competitor for the same reason ([EduGeek T4](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/208957-spiceworks-helpdesk/)).
- One school left Parago because staff kept emailing technicians directly. Forwarded mail then logged the ticket under the technician's name, so logging forwarded mail under the original sender became a hard requirement for its replacement ([EduGeek T2](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/212531-free-helpdesk-recommendations/)).
- Microsoft's retirement of basic authentication for mailboxes is the most-discussed osTicket issue (107 comments). Schools were still setting up osTicket on Microsoft 365 mailboxes in September 2026 ([osTicket issues](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:osTicket/osTicket+is:issue+sort:reactions-%2B1-desc&per_page=30); [EduGeek T10](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/226668-freshdesk-upcoming-but-undocumented-end-of-free-plan/)).
- There is a counter-view: one school deliberately takes tickets only through its portal so staff must give a room number, which is exactly what EduHelpdesk's tile launcher enforces ([EduGeek T1 p2](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/219505-helpdesk-options/page/2/)).

**Single sign-on** is less contested:

- A 19-school trust that runs on Google made Google sign-in mandatory ([EduGeek T9](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223860-helpdesk-recommendations-to-replace-jira-service-management/)).
- Halo was praised for Azure sign-in with user sync ([EduGeek T5](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/214671-mat-helpdesk-recommendations/)).
- SSO is the longest-running Spiceworks Cloud feature request: open since 2017, with 117 replies ([Spiceworks feature requests](https://community.spiceworks.com/tag/spiceworks-cloud-help-desk-chd-feature-request/752/l/top?period=all)).

The human problem behind both is adoption. In a 23-reply thread, technicians said staff still grab them in corridors and email them directly, and that only SLT backing plus fast service on logged tickets changes that ([EduGeek T12](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/205757-getting-staff-to-log-tickets/)). A tool cannot fix that culture, but it can make logging easier. At Michigan State University, a help button on lectern PCs and QR codes on teaching carts produced **25% of 1,749 tickets** within the first weeks ([MSU IT](https://tech.msu.edu/news/2024/12/it-help-button-and-qr-code-saving-time-reducing-stress-in-the-classroom)). Teachers have even less slack. A service-design study for the New South Wales education department found teachers cannot wait on calls or fill in forms between lessons. Being told a ticket was "resolved" when it had only been passed to another team destroyed trust, and the department made trust its main satisfaction measure ([Good HCD](https://goodhcd.com/portfolio/how-service-design-improved-support-for-nsw-schools/)). About **20% of English school IT staff work alone** ([SalamanderSoft/ANME](https://www.salamandersoft.co.uk/blog/anme-sponsor/)), so whatever gets built has to run with almost no administration.

International reviewers broadly agree with the UK picture. Education reviewers most often praise ease of use and tickets linked to device records, and most often complain about search, inflexible reporting, buggy mobile apps and price ([G2 Incident IQ](https://www.g2.com/products/incident-iq/reviews); [Capterra Incident IQ](https://capterra.com/p/186247/Incident-IQ/reviews/)). District leaders want trend reports by school and device model and the evidence to justify budgets. They reject being told to build their own reports in Power BI ([Spiceworks Community](https://community.spiceworks.com/search?q=school%20district%20help%20desk%20ticketing&order=latest)). CoSN's 2026 survey found **58% of US districts understaffed** for supporting teaching technology ([GovTech/CoSN](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/cosn-report-cybersecurity-is-top-concern-ai-guardrails-needed)). AI in school helpdesks is driven by vendors. Incident IQ claims its February 2026 assistant cut average resolution from five days to one, but publishes no method ([Incident IQ](https://www.incidentiq.com/newsroom/product-updates/ai-ticketing-assistant-k-12)). AI was barely mentioned in the school reviews read, and the one UK thread on AI agents was split: one poster saw them as FAQs reinvented, another used one only out of hours ([EduGeek T11](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/226464-ai-agents/)).

## Twenty-nine candidates scored: sign-in first, AI and billing last

### How the ranking was derived

Each candidate feature was scored on three axes. DfE and legal weight counts twice: it is the focus of this review, and it is the only axis an inspector, auditor or insurer can test. The score is twice the DfE weight plus demand plus competitor prevalence, out of 12. Ties are broken first by DfE weight, then by demand, then by my judgement of value for effort, explained in each item's text. An owner decision never lowered a rank; items the owner ruled out are labelled instead.

The three researchers' own shortlists disagreed. The DfE researcher led with a complete asset register, the UK practitioner researcher with cost and email, and the UK product researcher with device sync. The scoring reconciles them openly, but it is a judgement. A demand-first weighting would push email and multi-school support up and the evidence registers down.

| Axis | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DfE and legal weight (×2) | Statutory duty, or DfE says must, now or should already | Explicit expectation with no immediate deadline | Indirect support | None |
| UK and international demand | 6 or more recent EduGeek threads | 3–5 threads, or roughly 10 or more international mentions | 1–2 threads, or a recurring international request | Not voiced |
| Competitor prevalence | Standard everywhere, including free tools | Common in school-specific or paid tools | One or two products | None found |

Effort levels:

- **Low:** extends existing pages, fields or settings.
- **Medium:** a new page or module that reuses an existing part of the app (the scheduler, onboarding checklists, the SLA clock, custom attributes).
- **High:** needs an external integration, a setup step at each school, or an architectural change.

### Ranks 1–2: identity and intake

**Sign-in with the school's Microsoft 365 or Google account** ranks first because it is the only candidate with full marks on all three axes. Seven recent UK threads ask for SSO, MFA or user sync. OpenID Connect is a top Snipe-IT request (45 votes), and SSO has been open on the Spiceworks board since 2017 ([EduGeek T9](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223860-helpdesk-recommendations-to-replace-jira-service-management/); [Snipe-IT issues](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:grokability/snipe-it+is:issue+sort:reactions-%2B1-desc&per_page=40); [Spiceworks feature requests](https://community.spiceworks.com/tag/spiceworks-cloud-help-desk-chd-feature-request/752/l/top?period=all)). It closes three DfE gaps:

- **MFA:** the cyber standard requires MFA on staff accounts that reach cloud services or reach on-site systems remotely. EduHelpdesk portal users have their own passwords and no two-factor sign-in ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard); [feature inventory](../research_notes/School%20helpdesk%20comparison%20and%20priorities/eduhelpdesk_current_features.md)).
- **Password self-reset:** the IT support standard suggests it, and portal users currently cannot do it.
- **Leavers:** the cyber standard expects accounts to be disabled as soon as someone leaves. Directory sync would deactivate leavers' portal accounts without manual work.

Cyber Essentials raises the stakes. Under the v3.3 question set used from 27 April 2026, an assessment fails automatically if a cloud service offers MFA and it is not switched on for all users ([Forensic Control](https://forensiccontrol.com/news/cyber-essentials-v3-3-2026-update/)). If EduHelpdesk moves to Azure, as planned, portal accounts with no MFA option become exactly the exposure the standard describes. The blocker is not code: each school has to create an app registration. A guided setup is therefore the deliverable that matters most.

**Email-to-ticket** ranks second, and it is **previously ruled out by the owner**. The 2026-09-21 interview recorded that requester updates should be "nothing automatic" and that the mail system was unknown ([feature inventory](../research_notes/School%20helpdesk%20comparison%20and%20priorities/eduhelpdesk_current_features.md)). The evidence strongly favours revisiting that decision:

- Email intake is the third most common UK theme and drove the Spiceworks exodus ([EduGeek T4](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/208957-spiceworks-helpdesk/)).
- The IT support standard names email as an example channel ([IT support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support)).
- Civica, VIZOR and Desk365 all offer it. Desk365's UK customer runs about 70 schools on it after outgrowing osTicket ([Civica](https://www.civica.com/en-us/sector-pages/education/software-multi-academy-trusts/school-operations-and-compliance-software/); [VIZOR](https://www.vizor.cloud/school-help-desk-software/); [Desk365](https://www.desk365.io/customers/cts-uk/)).

The owner's privacy reasoning is about outbound messages: notification text stays generic because classroom screens are projected. That reasoning does not apply to inbound intake. A reader for one shared mailbox, using Microsoft Graph with the rank-1 app registration, would meet most of the demand without changing the outbound "nothing automatic" stance. Google schools would need a Gmail equivalent.

### Ranks 3–10: DfE evidence that mostly reuses existing engines

**Asset compliance fields (3)** are the cheapest high-value item, because EduHelpdesk already has lifespan-based replacement dates, a review list, and disposal date, method and proceeds. The standards ask for a few more things:

- The cyber standard wants each device's operating-system end-of-support date in the asset register ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)).
- Plan technology asks schools to track when assets become unsupported and to review the register every term ([Plan technology](https://plan-technology-for-your-school.education.gov.uk/digital-technology-asset-register)).
- The leadership standard wants a device's age recorded separately from how long the school has owned it, because kit may be second-hand ([leadership](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/digital-leadership-and-governance-core-standard)).
- The laptops standard requires WEEE and data-destruction certificates at disposal ([laptops](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/laptops-desktops-and-tablets)).
- NCSC wants disposal firms certified to schemes such as ADISA or CAS-S, and the waste code says to keep transfer notes for two years, or three for hazardous waste ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/secure-sanitisation-storage-media); [Defra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/waste-duty-of-care-code-of-practice/waste-duty-of-care-code-of-practice)).
- The register is also insurance evidence, because RPA cover excludes unexplained disappearance or inventory shortage ([RPA rules](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)).

Demand is modest; disposal and sale records are a top-five Snipe-IT request with 51 votes ([Snipe-IT issues](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:grokability/snipe-it+is:issue+sort:reactions-%2B1-desc&per_page=40)).

**Recurring compliance tasks (4)** carry the strongest legal driver on the list. KCSIE 2026, in force since 1 September 2026, says the filtering and monitoring review must happen at least once every academic year. The SLT lead runs it with the DSL and IT support. It must check filtering on every internet-connected device in every relevant location, and a record of the checks must be kept ([KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)). The filtering standard says what that record should hold: when each check happened, who did it, what was tested and what followed. It also requires SLT approval for temporary exceptions ([filtering and monitoring](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard)). The cyber standard adds termly logged restore tests and firewall reviews ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)).

EduHelpdesk's asset register already lists every device by type and location, so it could generate the check rows itself. UK technicians did not ask for recurring tasks. Internationally, though, persistent checklists drew 60 replies on the Spiceworks board (and Spiceworks then put them in its paid tier), and GLPI 11 added recurring changes ([Spiceworks feature requests](https://community.spiceworks.com/tag/spiceworks-cloud-help-desk-chd-feature-request/752/l/top?period=all); [Omnicom](https://www.omnicom.digital/en/2025/11/17/discover-the-new-glpi-11/)). It ranks above the contracts register, which scored the same, because its duties apply now under statutory guidance.

**The contracts and licence register (5)** answers the leadership standard's request for a register of licences, subscriptions and support contracts, with values and a list of approved apps. The cyber standard asks for licence expiry dates to be recorded and reviewed every term, and the IT support standard for a record of every support service and who provides it ([leadership](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/digital-leadership-and-governance-core-standard); [Plan technology contracts](https://www.plan-technology-for-your-school.education.gov.uk/digital-technology-contracts-register); [IT support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support)). The filtering standard now treats a newly adopted generative-AI tool as a trigger for a fresh review, so an approved-apps list doubles as filtering evidence. AssetTap already sends expiry reminders for documents and contracts, and GLPI tracks contracts and licences ([AssetTap](https://assettap.co.uk/); [GLPI](https://www.glpi-project.org/en/features/)).

**Routing and escalation (6)** and **the knowledge base with a known-issues banner (7)** score the same and are standard in competitor products:

- **Routing and escalation:** portal tickets currently arrive with no team or technician ([feature inventory](../research_notes/School%20helpdesk%20comparison%20and%20priorities/eduhelpdesk_current_features.md)). Incident IQ and Manage1to1 route by location, asset, category or keyword, while Spiceworks cannot route at all ([Incident IQ](https://www.incidentiq.com/platform/automations); [Manage1to1](https://manage1to1.com/); [Siit](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/spiceworks-review)). The IT support standard asks schools to define how serious incidents are escalated, which EduHelpdesk cannot express today.
- **Knowledge base:** UK demand is lukewarm; one trust wanted it only for the minority of users who help themselves ([EduGeek T9](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223860-helpdesk-recommendations-to-replace-jira-service-management/)). The DfE case is explicit, though: the IT support standard asks for self-service and for plain-English guides on logging in, resetting passwords, common problems and raising requests ([IT support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support)). EduHelpdesk's Staff Guide is that last guide, and it still describes the old free-text form and says the system sends no notifications.
- **Stale help backfires:** the New South Wales study found outdated quick-reference PDFs and poor search pushed staff to ask colleagues instead ([Good HCD](https://goodhcd.com/portfolio/how-service-design-improved-support-for-nsw-schools/)).

**Offboarding (8)** and **approvals (9)** score full DfE weight but drew no user demand:

- **Offboarding:** the cyber standard expects a joiner, mover and leaver process agreed with HR, accounts disabled as soon as someone leaves, and accounts reviewed every term ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)). EduHelpdesk has the joiner half and a leaver check. Its onboarding engine (templates, task owners, day offsets) can carry the rest, which is the stage the owner already planned.
- **Approvals:** these show up in three standards. The IT support standard's request record includes an approver where needed. The cyber standard wants a member of SLT or a trustee to approve privilege changes before IT carries them out. The filtering standard wants exceptions approved and documented ([IT support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support); [filtering and monitoring](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard)). ManageEngine and FMX both offer approvals ([ManageEngine K-12](https://www.manageengine.com/products/service-desk/industry/help-desk-software-k12-schools.html); [FMX](https://www.gofmx.com/school-help-desk-software/)). Without email, approvers would have to act in the portal after a ping, which is the delivery risk to test.

**QR labels and stocktake (10)** were "not wanted yet" in the owner's asset interview. The evidence is moderate:

- **DfE:** the laptops standard asks schools to tag devices and record them in the register ([laptops](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/laptops-desktops-and-tablets)).
- **UK users:** one school leaving IRIS said it valued QR codes on mobile devices ([EduGeek T14](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/225733-it-asset-management-software/)).
- **UK competitors:** AssetTap's QR audits and stocktakes surface exceptions ([AssetTap](https://assettap.co.uk/)).
- **Self-hosted precedent:** Chalk shows a scan-to-reconcile audit can run on an ordinary barcode scanner that types like a keyboard, with no camera needed ([Chalk](https://github.com/usechalk/chalk)).
- **Intake:** QR room tags that open the portal with the room filled in are the closest match to Michigan State's button-and-QR result.

### Ranks 11–19: narrower gains, several of them cheap

**The cyber incident and breach register (11)** fills a complete gap:

- The cyber standard says incidents, attacks and near misses should be recorded in an internal system, and lists where to report them: Report Fraud, the DfE sector cyber team, the RPA or insurer, NCSC for major incidents, and the ICO within 72 hours for high-risk breaches ([cyber](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)).
- UK GDPR requires every breach to be documented with its facts, effects and remedial action, whether or not it is reported ([UK GDPR Art 33](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)).
- Trusts must report fraud or theft over £5,000 (single or cumulative in a year) to DfE ([ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)).
- The need is common: the 2025 Cyber Security Breaches Survey found **60% of secondaries and 44% of primaries** reported a cyber incident in the past year ([dataprotection.education](https://dataprotection.education/infosec/what-school-leaders-need-to-know-about-the-dfes-new-cyber-security-hub)).

Much of this can be set up today: a restricted category, category-scoped custom attributes and a 72-hour SLA.

**Backup hardening (12)** ranks just below because the basics already exist. EduHelpdesk takes nightly, integrity-checked backups. They are unencrypted, though, and there is no record of termly restore tests, which both the cyber standard and the RPA backup condition expect ([RPA via DfE Cyber Security Hub](https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/risk-protection-arrangement)). The need is real: only 47% of primaries meet DfE's backup guideline ([DfE TiSS](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692834a6ce50d215cae9610e/Technology_in_schools_survey_2024_to_2025_research_report.pdf)).

**The annual IT support review pack (13)** is the IT support standard's own output: a written yearly review for governors, drawn from support records, that identifies busy periods and the systems causing most requests ([IT support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support)). It scores lower than the DfE researcher's shortlist suggested, for two reasons. UK technicians voice little demand for reports, and one small school said outright it did not want statistics ([EduGeek T2](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/212531-free-helpdesk-recommendations/)). And only AssetTap among competitors positions itself on DfE. The pack also needs the volume-over-time view the owner previously did not choose. If governors start asking for it, this item moves up quickly, because most of its numbers already exist in the seven report pages.

The next four items are each small and low effort:

- **Term dates (14):** these fix SLA overdue counts over holidays, and they make statutory clocks possible: FOI's 20 school days, and SARs, which cannot be extended because it is the school holidays ([ICO FOI for schools](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/learning-resources-and-training-videos/advice-for-schools-on-the-foia-and-responding-to-requests-for-information/); [DfE SARs](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/dealing-with-subject-access-requests-sars)).
- **The privacy and accessibility pack (15):** this replaces a privacy page that is still the ASP.NET template text. It also gives schools what DfE asks them to collect from EdTech before adopting it: where data is stored, the security measures, and deletion arrangements ([DfE EdTech](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/procuring-educational-technology-edtech)). And it logs which records each retention run destroyed and who authorised it, as the FOIA requires ([DfE record keeping](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management)).
- **First-response tracking (16), previously ruled out by the owner:** the IT support standard explicitly separates being responded to from being resolved, and one UK technician won staff over with an informal 15-minute response to urgent tickets ([EduGeek T12](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/205757-getting-staff-to-log-tickets/)).
- **"Is it fixed?" (17):** this implements the standard's own example of a further action for staff, and avoids the false-resolution distrust found in the New South Wales study.

**Departments and multi-school support (18)** has the most demand of any item below rank 2: six recent UK threads. A 19-school trust wants several helpdesks with one manager dashboard, and a UK managed-service provider moved about 70 schools off osTicket after outgrowing it ([EduGeek T9](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223860-helpdesk-recommendations-to-replace-jira-service-management/); [Desk365](https://www.desk365.io/customers/cts-uk/)). It ranks here only because DfE asks nothing of it directly and full tenancy fights EduHelpdesk's single-instance, in-memory design. Departments within one school are the cheap first step.

**Intune and Google Admin sync (19)** is the UK product researcher's top gap and is standard among competitors ([Allied ESM](https://alliedesm.com/halo/integrations); [Freshservice](https://support.freshservice.com/support/solutions/articles/50000001055-ms-intune-integration-with-freshservice)). No UK technician thread asked for it directly. Writing changes back to the device-management system needs care: Incident IQ users report that its sync overwrote a "lost" status with Google's "disabled" ([Incident IQ community](https://community.incidentiq.com/integrations-73/can-i-deprovision-and-disable-devices-in-iiq-with-google-devices-integration-386)).

### Ranks 20–29: low demand, low DfE weight, or both

The **safeguarding signpost tile (20)** and the **training and RPA readiness register (21)** score full DfE weight, but nobody asked for them and no competitor offers them:

- **Safeguarding tile:** KCSIE says staff should act on concerns immediately and speak to the DSL, and that child-protection records belong in a separate, secure file ([KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)). A helpdesk should therefore point people to that route rather than collect details. A tile that shows the DSL's name and route without creating a detailed ticket is close to a configuration change and could be done today.
- **Training and RPA register:** RPA cyber cover requires members to show, at claim time, that staff and governors with IT access completed NCSC training before the membership year began, that the school is registered with Police CyberAlarm, and that it has a response plan ([RPA rules](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)). A dated record per person is cheap insurance evidence.

**Loan agreements (22)** draw on a Derbyshire template that records device, condition, dates, signatures and loss liability ([Education Data Hub](https://educationdatahub.org.uk/resources/device-loan-agreement/)). Snipe-IT and One To One Plus offer signatures ([Snipe-IT](https://snipeitapp.com/features); [One To One Plus](https://onetooneplus.com/pricing/)).

**Phone-first technician use (23)** matters less than the 4-thread demand suggests, because EduHelpdesk already has a phone layout. The 19-school trust lead was not bothered about an app ([EduGeek T9](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/223860-helpdesk-recommendations-to-replace-jira-service-management/)).

**Auto-chase and auto-close (24)**, previously ruled out, answers a real complaint that staff never reply or confirm fixes ([EduGeek T12](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/205757-getting-staff-to-log-tickets/)). Rank 17 already covers most of it without anything happening automatically.

The last five items fall to the bottom for different reasons:

- **MIS roster sync (25):** a genuine UK differentiator, since no UK helpdesk found offers Wonde-style sync, even though Wonde connects 30,000+ schools ([Wonde](https://www.wonde.com/)). Only two recent threads asked for it.
- **AI triage (26):** demand in UK forums is mixed and there is no independent outcome data. DfE's generative AI product safety standards target learner-facing products, not helpdesks ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-standards/generative-ai-product-safety-standards)).
- **Damage charging (27):** a US K-12 staple ([Incident IQ Fee Tracker](https://www.incidentiq.com/apps/fee-tracker)). It depends on the part costs the owner rejected, and on DfE charging rules that were not verified; in the UK, parental-contribution schemes run through separate firms such as edde ([edde](https://edde.education/)).
- **Canned replies (28) and time tracking (29):** both previously ruled out, and the evidence is weak. The UK research found only general "templates" mentions and one trust's per-site hourly billing ([EduGeek T6](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/206184-ticketing-system/); [EduGeek T12](https://www.edugeek.net/forums/topic/205757-getting-staff-to-log-tickets/)).

Whatever their rank, five items are quick wins:

- the safeguarding tile
- term dates
- the privacy page
- an end-of-support date field
- a refreshed Staff Guide

## Conflicting dates and a blocked Reddit limit certainty

Several sources disagree:

- **IT support standard publication date:** the gov.uk content API records the section as added on 17 November 2025, while Smoothwall dates publication to 15 December 2025 ([gov.uk content API](https://www.gov.uk/api/content/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support); [Smoothwall](https://smoothwall.com/resources/new-dfe-it-support-standards)). This report treats the gov.uk log as authoritative.
- **Page addresses:** researchers reached the same standards at two different addresses. The IT support standard appeared at [/it-support](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support) and [/it-support-standards-for-schools-and-colleges](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support-standards-for-schools-and-colleges). The cyber standard appeared at /cyber-security-core-standard and /cyber-security-standards-for-schools-and-colleges. Either the pages were renamed or both addresses are live; links should be rechecked before they are shared.
- **What the September 2026 update changed:** one vendor credits it with faster patching and immutable backups, but the gov.uk change log shows the September changes only added safeguarding text and a Cyber Security Hub link. The main cyber change was on 24 June 2026, to align with Cyber Essentials 2026 ([Lead IT Services](https://www.leaditservices.co.uk/news/dfe-six-core-digital-standards/); [GOV.UK updates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/updates)).
- **KCSIE paragraph numbers:** a safeguarding site places the filtering review at paragraph 168, but the primary text has it at paragraphs 173–175 ([safeguard-hub](https://safeguard-hub.org/articles/kcsie-2026-changes/); [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)).
- **Spiceworks pricing dates:** charging was announced on 21 April 2025 and took effect on 1 June 2025 (first planned for 1 July). One secondary source dates the seat cap to May 2025. The desktop edition's end of life (31 December 2021) and an earlier end of development (2018) are compatible.
- **The RPA rules PDF:** one researcher could not parse it, while another quoted it. The quoted four cyber conditions match the DfE Cyber Security Hub, so this report relies on them.
- **Guesses about EduHelpdesk:** other notes made guesses that the code-based inventory corrects. Parts link to asset types, not to individual assets. No asset label view exists; the only label is the ticket label. The shipped README, Technical Guide and Staff Guide still describe nine permission toggles, no notifications and the old free-text form, so any comparison built from those documents would undersell the app ([feature inventory](../research_notes/School%20helpdesk%20comparison%20and%20priorities/eduhelpdesk_current_features.md)).

There are also gaps in the sources:

- **Reddit:** it was blocked for both the UK and international researchers, so there is no r/sysadmin, r/k12sysadmin or UK Reddit evidence.
- **EduGeek bias:** practitioner evidence rests on 18 EduGeek threads and the Spiceworks Community. EduGeek over-represents secondaries and trusts with in-house teams, while most primaries use managed service providers.
- **Incident IQ:** its own site returned access errors, and its idea board and G2's theme counts sit behind logins.
- **No usage data:** no UK survey of helpdesk software was found, nor any source on what governors actually ask IT to report.
- **AI outcomes:** no independent measurement of helpdesk AI was found.
- **DfE templates:** the asset and contracts register spreadsheets were not opened, so field lists come from page text.
- **Unverified:** Police CyberAlarm's status, DfE rules on charging parents for damage, and the start date of the Data (Use and Access) Act's SAR change. Many question marks in the competitor table were also not checked.
- **Method limits:** demand counts are forum mentions, not market share, and the scoring weights are a judgement.
- **EduHelpdesk itself:** real Teams delivery and desktop notifications are untested, and there is no recorded WCAG audit or load test.
- **Scope:** everything here applies to England only.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes what "behind" means for EduHelpdesk. It is not behind on ticketing: it beats Spiceworks Cloud on SLAs and roles, and its search and ticket merge avoid the basic complaints made against Incident IQ. It lags on two foundations, identity and intake, and on turning its records into evidence a governor, auditor or insurer would accept. The two foundations share a prerequisite. One Microsoft Entra app registration, or its Google equivalent, unlocks sign-in (rank 1), mailbox intake (rank 2) and Intune sync (rank 19). A guided "connect to Microsoft 365" setup is therefore the single most useful piece of work, because it removes the blocker the owner has already recorded. The planned Azure move raises the stakes. Once EduHelpdesk is reachable as a cloud service, DfE's MFA requirement clearly covers portal staff accounts, so portal MFA through single sign-on should arrive before or with the move.

The DfE tier is cheaper than its length suggests, because most of it reuses parts of the app that already exist:

- category-scoped custom attributes and hour-based SLAs for an incident log
- the onboarding engine for leavers
- the nightly scheduler for recurring checks
- the review list for end-of-support alerts

It is also distinctive. The IT support standard is less than a year old, only a quarter of IT leads who know the standards say they meet them all, and no general ITSM vendor maps its features to them. A free tool that prints a governor-ready evidence pack would hold ground that AssetTap only gestures at. That pack would cover the KCSIE filtering record, the backup restore log, the incident register and the annual IT support review. The owner's no-email stance is the one decision the evidence most clearly contests. An inbound-only mailbox reader would keep outbound updates "nothing automatic" while meeting UK technicians' third-biggest request.
