# DfE Digital and Technology Standards (England) - requirements a school IT helpdesk / asset register / ITSM tool can help meet or evidence

Research date: 2026-10-03. All gov.uk standard pages fetched on this date show "Last updated 16 September 2026" (that date is the collection-wide update; individual sections changed on the dates in the change log below).

Method note: gov.uk pages were read with a fetch tool that summarises; phrases shown in quotation marks were returned as quotes by that tool and are believed accurate, but any phrase that will be reproduced in a user-facing document should be spot-checked against the live page. The digital leadership asset-register list was requested and returned verbatim. The RPA membership rules PDF (September 2026) could not be parsed, so RPA details come from the DfE Cyber Security Hub page plus secondary sources.

## 1. What is the full current list of DfE digital and technology standards, which are core, and what are the target dates?

### Takeaway
There are 12 standards: 6 "core" standards (broadband, wireless, switching, digital leadership and governance, filtering and monitoring, cyber security) that schools "should be working towards meeting" by 2030, plus 6 others (cloud solutions, digital accessibility, IT support, laptops/desktops/tablets, network cabling, servers and storage). The newest is the IT support standard, added on 17 November 2025. The 2030 date is the headline target, but the filtering and monitoring pages and parts of the cyber security pages say schools "should already be meeting" them, and backups "must" be done "now".

The core 6 are broadband internet, wireless network, network switching, digital leadership and governance, filtering and monitoring, and cyber security. The additional standards are cloud solutions, digital accessibility, IT support, laptops/desktops/tablets, network cabling, and servers and storage. ### Inferences
- The 2030 date covers the infrastructure core standards (broadband, wireless, switching) and leadership. For filtering and monitoring, and for most of cyber security, DfE wording ("already", "must ... now") means schools are expected to comply today. A helpdesk that helps evidence these two areas therefore meets an immediate need, not a 2030 one.
- The standards change several times a year (11 dated changes in Jan-Sep 2026). Any compliance checklist built into EduHelpdesk should be editable data rather than hard-coded text, and should show a "last checked against DfE on" date.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland have equivalent standards. These DfE standards apply to England only, and no devolved-nation sources were checked.
- I found no gov.uk record of a 2025 consultation tied to the 2030 date. Only one secondary source mentions it.

## 2. For each standard, which "how to meet" items imply a tool capability (asset register, lifecycle, disposal, admin accounts, MFA, incidents, patching, backups, joiners/movers/leavers, filtering reviews, roles, strategy, accessibility, energy)?

### Takeaway
The standards repeatedly require records that a helpdesk/asset tool can hold or report on:
- an asset register (with prescribed fields, including OS end-of-support dates and secure-disposal dates, reviewed termly)
- a contracts/licence register (expiry dates, reviewed termly)
- an internal cyber incident log (including near misses) with an external-reporting checklist
- an admin-account register with SLT approval of privilege changes
- a joiner/mover/leaver process agreed with HR, with termly account reviews
- a 14-day patch log for CVSS ≥7.0 vulnerabilities
- a termly backup and restore test log
- termly firewall-rule reviews
- annual training/AUP sign-up records
- a log of filtering checks and an annual filtering review record
- SLT-approved filtering exceptions
- documented filtering/monitoring incidents with actions and outcomes

### Cited Findings

**Digital leadership and governance (core)** — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/digital-leadership-and-governance-core-standard)
- Roles: assign an SLT member responsible for digital technology (the "SLT digital lead"). Governors "should also consider assigning a digital link role". The SLT digital lead is accountable for "reviewing the effectiveness of IT support to inform decision making and taking action, when necessary".
- Three registers are expected: a contracts register, an asset register (owned by the SLT digital lead and kept up to date by IT support) and an information asset register (owned by the DPO).
- Asset register contents, verbatim: "what equipment you have, asset numbers, serial numbers, who it is assigned to, where it is within the school or college, when it was purchased, how old it is – this may be different to how long you have owned it, as it may be second-hand equipment, when it is due for review so that you can consider a replacement or upgrade, date it was securely disposed of".
- Register update cadence: "You should already be updating your registers every time something changes". Registers should be reviewed ahead of the next financial planning cycle.
- The contracts register should hold licences, subscriptions, and broadband, IT support and technology-provider contracts, plus a "List of approved apps" and the value of contracts "to monitor spend".
- Include digital technology in the disaster recovery and business continuity plans. The DR plan should be "Tested annually (at a minimum)", printed in hard copy, and should name the DR team and key contacts.
- Digital technology strategy: "minimum 2-year", reviewed annually, covering "Contracts and assets", "Current and committed digital technology spend", risks/DR, and staff/student training needs. The vision should be "sustainable and minimise the impact on the environment". The SEND lead identifies "accessibility, diversity and inclusion needs".
- DfE templates exist for the contracts register, asset register and information asset register.

**Plan technology asset register guidance (DfE)** — [Plan technology: digital technology asset register](https://plan-technology-for-your-school.education.gov.uk/digital-technology-asset-register)
- Asset types to cover: "laptops, desktops and tablets, interactive whiteboards, servers and routers, hardware firewall, 3D printers, phones, network cabling".
- What to track: "how many assets you have, when assets were purchased, reviewed and are due to expire, become unsupported or go out of warranty, the total value of your assets, where assets are located, who your assets are assigned to".
- Review cadence: the register should be "reviewed by the person responsible for your IT equipment every term, and updated when any changes take place".
- Template: "Digital technology asset register template.xlsx, 106 KB, last updated 10 January 2025".

**Plan technology contracts register guidance** — [Plan technology: contracts register](https://www.plan-technology-for-your-school.education.gov.uk/digital-technology-contracts-register)
- Scope: IT support, broadband, MIS, telephony, filtering/monitoring, "maintenance and support contracts for the servicing and repair of equipment", leasing, "licences for software", SSL certificates and security monitoring services.
- Cadence: "Your SLT digital lead should check your digital technology contracts register every term". Template: XLSX, 83 KB, 10 Jan 2025.
- An information asset register template also exists — [Plan technology: information asset register](https://plan-technology-for-your-school.education.gov.uk/information-asset-register)

**Cyber security (core)**: 7 sub-standards — [GOV.UK cyber security](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)

1. **Cyber risk assessment**, "every year" and "revisited every term":
   - "review digital technology assets and any related cyber security risk"
   - "check all digital technology is licensed, supported and updated"
   - "complete a record of processing activities (ROPA)"
   - "assess staff access and permissions to systems and data"
   - "keep documentation on your network up to date – this should include network diagrams, changes that are made, settings and IP addressing information"
   - "discuss the level of logging required"
   - Cyber risks go "in the risk register" and a "regularly tested business continuity plan"
   - "put a cyber response plan in place" (also an RPA condition)
   - If IT support is outsourced, "discuss with them how they are meeting the requirements"; consider asking whether suppliers hold Cyber Essentials/Plus; carry out supplier assessments when procuring.
   - The page also states: "The end of support dates for each device's operating system should be recorded in the asset register".
2. **Cyber awareness plan**:
   - An acceptable use policy that anyone with network/data access must "sign up to"
   - Training "at least annually, or more regularly if there is a known cyber risk", with topics including phishing/social engineering, passwords, MFA and "how to report a cyber incident"
   - RPA members "must evidence that the relevant users have undertaken the free National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) training"
   - "at least one current governor or trustee" should be trained
3. **Anti-malware and firewall**:
   - Keep firewall firmware updated, "checked termly"
   - Change the default admin password and use MFA on the firewall admin interface
   - "document and review why inbound traffic has been permitted through the firewall – this should be done on a termly basis at a minimum"
   - Anti-malware on all devices, "centrally managed, actively monitored"; "send malware alerts to IT support who will then investigate the issue"
   - "prohibit the use of USB storage devices by default, unless for a specific need"
   - "check and approve all current and future applications"
4. **User accounts and access privileges**:
   - Unique credentials; lockout after "no more than 10 unsuccessful attempts"
   - "MFA must be enabled for all staff accounts with access to cloud services or remote access to on-site systems" and "MFA must be enabled for IT administrative accounts"
   - Account lifecycle: "creating accounts only when required"; "disabling accounts as soon as someone leaves their role"; "creating and managing a process with human resources ... to deal with joiners, leavers and those moving roles"; "review accounts with your business professionals or the finance team every term"
   - Admin accounts: "global or administrative accounts are not used for routine business"; "agree a process for handling administrative accounts so that a member of SLT or a trustee approves any changes to access levels or privileges before IT support can action the change"; "make sure SLT have access to a dedicated administrative account – this will only be needed in an emergency"
5. **Licensing and updates**:
   - "licence expiry dates are recorded in the contracts register"; "digital technology end-of-support dates are captured in the asset register"
   - "IT support must complete vulnerability fixes for operating systems, applications and firmware within 14 days of the fix being released" where the vendor rates the fix critical/high or the vulnerability has "CVSS v3.1 ... base score of 7.0 or above"
   - "isolate devices where high risk patches are unavailable"
   - DfE may issue update instructions, which "IT support should then apply ... within 5 working days of notification"
   - "At the end of every term, IT support and the business professionals or the finance team should review the contracts register"
6. **Backups**:
   - "at least 3 backup copies of important data, on at least 2 separate devices", "at least one ... off-site"
   - "make sure that backups are immutable"
   - "backups should never be taken to anyone's home"
   - "test and log your backups termly or if there is a significant change, this should include the ability to recover and restore"
   - Have a policy on restore-test frequency; review the plan annually
   - Backing up to this level is "currently a condition of cover" for RPA
7. **Report cyber attacks**:
   - On an alert, action the cyber incident response plan: contain, notify, "capture information on the risk", investigate, and "report the potential incident or attack to the governing body or trustees"
   - "Any incidents, attacks or near misses should be recorded in an internal incident report or system"
   - External reporting: the RPA/cyber insurer; Report Fraud (0300 123 2040); the DfE sector cyber team (Sector.Incidentreporting@education.gov.uk); NCSC if there is long-term closure, multi-school closure or serious financial damage; the ICO "within 72 hours" for a high-risk data breach; Jisc for FE

**Filtering and monitoring (core)** — [GOV.UK filtering & monitoring](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard)
- Roles: governing body/proprietor; an SLT member plus a governor; the DSL; and IT support (in-house or third party), who "complete actions following concerns" and run checks.
- "Review your filtering and monitoring provision at least annually". Extra reviews are triggered by a safeguarding risk, a change in working practice (remote access/BYOD), new technology (including new devices or generative AI tools), major updates or config changes. "You should record the results of the review and document any actions taken."
- Filtering checks must be "completed and recorded as part of your filtering and monitoring review process". Keep "a log of your checks" recording "when the check took place", "who did the check", "what they tested or checked" and "resulting actions".
- Temporary exceptions (e.g. for IT admin updates) need the responsible SLT member to "approve and document it".
- Staff should report to the appropriate person (DSL/IT) when:
  - they witness or suspect unsuitable material has been accessed
  - they can access material they believe should be restricted
  - teaching shows unusual filtering activity
  - filtering systems fail or are abused
  - blocking unreasonably restricts teaching
  - abbreviations or misspellings let users reach restricted material
- The filtering system must identify, as a minimum, "device name or ID, IP address, and where possible, the individual; the time and date of attempted access; the search term or content being blocked".
- Monitoring plan:
  - at least weekly reports, and immediate reports for high-risk incidents
  - "There should be a documented process for recording incidents that includes what action was taken and the outcomes"
  - users must be identifiable "including guest accounts where possible"
  - a DPIA for technical monitoring
- Schools "should consider whether a filtering and monitoring solution that can handle real-time, dynamic, personalised and AI-generated content is required". Schools introducing generative AI should refer to the "Generative AI: product safety standards".

**IT support**: see section 3 below.

**Laptops, desktops and tablets** — [GOV.UK laptops standard](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/laptops-desktops-and-tablets)
- Devices should be "centrally managed"; MDM for portable devices (remote lock/wipe); "enterprise or education-grade operating systems"; accessibility features "not blocked by security policies".
- Minimum specification for new purchases:
  - manufacturer support and security patches: tablets 3 years, laptops/desktops 5 years
  - warranty: laptops/desktops 3 years, tablets 2 years
  - Wi-Fi at least 802.11ac Wave 2 (Wi-Fi 6 recommended)
  - tablet screen at least 9.7 inches
  - IT support reviews minimum requirements annually
- Use "labels or tags to keep track of them when you buy them and add them in an asset register"; record "up-to-date information about the device in the asset register".
- Energy: devices "switched off when not in use", "automatically power off" outside hours, Energy Star-rated purchases, repurpose before buying.
- Disposal: comply with WEEE; obtain "certificates for WEEE, disposal and destruction of data"; follow NCSC "secure sanitisation of storage media" guidance; ensure "the IT asset register is updated".
- The section was first added 16 Jan 2024 ([content API](https://www.gov.uk/api/content/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/laptops-desktops-and-tablets)).

**Servers and storage** — [GOV.UK servers & storage](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/servers-and-storage)
- IT support should "monitor performance and alert you if a component or service fails". Keep "valid manufacturer warranties and support agreements", "a process to make sure that patches and firmware upgrades are up to date", and "a process to replace servers ... when they're approaching end of warranty, end of support, or end of life".
- UPS with at least 30 minutes run-time.
- "review your existing systems and services ... at least each term, to make sure that they are secure"; a DPIA for servers storing personal data; a documented "user account creation, approval and removal process"; documented data-breach roles.
- Energy: power saving, ENERGY STAR. The server room should be a locked, cooled room with a dedicated power supply.

**Network switching (core)** — [GOV.UK switching](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/network-switching-core-standard)
- Manufacturer warranty/support including firmware "providing 5 years of cover as a minimum". "Equipment that is no longer able to receive firmware and security updates should be replaced".
- "Any administrative accounts that have access to make configuration changes, must be secure and fully documented". Updates set to automatic. Critical core switches on UPS. Target by 2030.

**Wireless network (core)** — [GOV.UK wireless](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/wireless-network-core-standard)
- Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) as the minimum "when upgrading"; schools upgrade only when the network no longer meets needs (17 Apr 2026 change).
- About "one per classroom" access points; heat mapping; a central management tool that monitors and alerts.
- Warranty/support including firmware; WPA3; VLANs; guest segregation; "MFA for privileged users". Target by 2030.

**Broadband internet (core)** — [GOV.UK broadband](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/broadband-internet-core-standard)
- Full fibre: primary at least 100 Mbps down / 30 Mbps up; secondary/FE "capacity to deliver 1Gbps download and upload".
- A backup connection of a different type with automatic failover; a firewall and content filtering. Change at contract end. Target by 2030.

**Cloud solutions** — [GOV.UK cloud](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cloud-solutions)
- A DPIA for personal data; data sharing agreements; data held in the UK/EU unless UK GDPR compliant.
- "one centrally managed account with one log in for each user"; "have agreed, documented processes in place to manage the addition and removal of users".
- Understand the provider's backup, using 3-2-1 for critical data.

**Digital accessibility** — [GOV.UK digital accessibility](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/digital-accessibility)
- Include accessibility needs in strategies and policies.
- Devices should support "text-to-speech and dictation", "caption settings", "zoom", work with assistive technology, and not have accessibility features "blocked by generic security policies".
- Request "accessibility statements" from suppliers. Provide alternative formats for communications. Train staff.

**Network cabling** — [GOV.UK cabling](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/network-cabling)
- Cat 6A copper (permanent link no more than 90 m) and 16-core OM4 fibre between buildings. The installer provides a "detailed test report"; "A minimum 20-year manufacturer's performance warranty". Little helpdesk relevance beyond recording warranties and test certificates.

### Inferences
These map the cited requirements to EduHelpdesk features. The status of existing features comes from the project's memory notes, not from the DfE sources.

**Asset register (highest evidence value):**
- Fields DfE names that a register should carry:
  - asset number
  - serial
  - assigned person
  - location
  - purchase date
  - age (separately from ownership time, for second-hand kit)
  - review/replacement-due date
  - warranty expiry
  - OS/vendor end-of-support date
  - value
  - secure-disposal date, plus WEEE/data-destruction certificate attachments
- Also needed: a termly "register reviewed" sign-off and a report of assets out of support or warranty.
- EduHelpdesk already has assets with warranty, statuses, a review list and disposal reporting. The likely gaps are end-of-support date, separate age, disposal certificates and a termly review attestation.

**Contracts/licence register:** supplier, type, value, start/end and licence expiry, renewal alerts, an approved-apps list, and a termly review sign-off. Cited by leadership, cyber and Plan technology. The existing projects/quotes and supplier data could seed it.

**Cyber incident log:** a ticket type for incidents, attacks and near misses, with:
- containment and investigation fields
- a "reported to governors" flag
- an external-reporting checklist with timestamps (RPA/insurer, Report Fraud, DfE sector team, NCSC, ICO within 72 hours, Jisc)
- links to the cyber response plan

**Joiner/mover/leaver workflow** with HR, with "disable account on leaving" tasks and a termly account review. EduHelpdesk has onboarding (joiners); offboarding (leavers) and movers are the DfE-relevant gap.

**Privileged-access register and approval workflow:** a list of admin accounts (including documented switch/firewall/wireless admin accounts), MFA status, and an SLT/trustee approval step before any privilege change. This maps neatly onto ticket approvals.

**Recurring compliance tasks/checklists** with evidence attachments, covering:
- termly backup restore tests
- termly firewall rule reviews
- termly firmware checks
- annual DR test
- annual F&M review
- a filtering-check log (when, who, what tested, actions)
- SLT-approved filtering exceptions

**Training and attestation records:** annual NCSC certificate per staff member and governor, and AUP sign-up.

**Patch/vulnerability tracking** against a 14-day SLA for CVSS ≥7.0, and a 5-working-day SLA for DfE instructions. This could reuse SLA timers on a "security update" ticket type.

**Safeguarding-sensitive ticket category** for filtering/monitoring concerns raised by staff (e.g. over-blocking, access to restricted content). It needs restricted visibility (DSL plus IT) and a recorded action and outcome.

### Gaps
- I did not see the column headers of the DfE asset register and contracts register XLSX templates. They were not downloaded, so exact template fields are unverified beyond the gov.uk/Plan technology text.
- The cyber page says to "discuss the level of logging required" but gives no retention period for security logs.
- The standards give no device refresh cycle in years, only support/warranty minimums and a "review date".

## 3. Is there a DfE standard specifically about IT technical support (service levels, contracts, in-house vs outsourced, helpdesk)? What does it say?

### Takeaway
Yes. The "IT support" standard (first published 17 November 2025; not a core standard) has 5 sub-standards. It explicitly recommends a "case management or helpdesk system to record and track requests" with defined fields, recorded response/resolution expectations, prioritisation by teaching impact, escalation, multiple channels, self-service, capacity reporting, an annual written IT support review for governors, a staff knowledge base and induction training. It sets no numeric SLA targets. It is the standard most directly aligned to EduHelpdesk.

### Cited Findings
All from [GOV.UK IT support standard](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support); first published 17 Nov 2025, "New section added", per the [content API](https://www.gov.uk/api/content/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/it-support).

**Standard 1: IT support helps you meet the standards.**
- Staff need "necessary qualifications and experience".
- Consider "relevant professional memberships or certifications", e.g. ITIL and Cyber Essentials.

**Standard 2: IT support actively maintains and improves technology in line with the digital strategy.**
- Maintain "registers of assets, software and IT support's own activities".
- Provide "technical information to help the business or finance team manage and update software licences".
- "Keep user and technical documentation up to date, including system configurations".
- Do "security patching, updating firmware and routine health checks".
- Ensure backups "are secure and restorable".
- Work with the DSL on filtering and monitoring; manage user accounts; respond to "day-to-day support requests".
- Keep an "up-to-date record of all IT support services" (providers and coverage).
- Planning: "Identify when technology needs replacing"; "Advise on the budget implications of upgrades or replacements".

**Standard 3: IT support is responsive and meets agreed service expectations.**
- Service expectations:
  - "Set and record clear expectations for how quickly issues will be responded to and resolved"
  - agree "how support requests will be prioritised—for example, giving higher priority to issues that affect teaching and learning"
  - define "how complex issues or serious incidents will be escalated"
  - consider how long the school "could continue to operate if a critical system ... was unavailable"
  - "Meet regularly to review how well the service is working"
- Channels:
  - "multiple ways to raise requests, such as phone, email and in person"
  - "Avoid making support requests through unofficial channels, such as instant messages"
  - in-person requests should be "recorded and tracked formally"
  - "clear operating hours, including support during school hours and arrangements for emergency cover"
  - "self-service options for common issues"
- Use a "case management or helpdesk system to record and track requests", recording:
  - "A description of the issue"
  - "The date and time the request was made"
  - "The requester's name and the approver if required"
  - "The issue's priority level"
  - "Actions taken to investigate and resolve the issue"
- Staff should be able to "check the status of their requests and see whether they need to take any further actions".
- Capacity:
  - "Review support records to see how well demand is being handled"
  - "Plan additional support for times of high demand, such as the start of the academic year or exam periods"
  - consider simple workload reducers "such as allowing staff to reset their own passwords"
  - consider a "hybrid approach" (in-house plus specialist providers)
- Reporting: "Use these records to monitor how IT support is meeting demand". Reports go to "governors or trust leaders as part of the yearly review". "Identify recurring problems, highlight outdated technology that may need replacing".

**Standard 4: Review IT support at least once a year**, alongside the digital strategy review.
- Led by the SLT digital lead, with IT support, staff feedback, the business manager, the head and governors.
- The review covers:
  - type of support
  - whether the record of IT support services is up to date
  - new needs
  - skills and capacity
  - budget
- It also looks at "Times of high demand", "Any technologies or systems causing a high volume of requests" and training needs.
- Contracts: review "contracts for any external IT support, including how well it's performing" and value for money; "value for money does not necessarily mean the lowest cost".
- "Summarise the review findings in writing" and share with the SLT, business manager and governors/trust board.

**Standard 5: Staff get clear guidance and training.**
- Induction training "when they join or change roles", plus refresher training covering MIS and safeguarding/behaviour platforms.
- Plain-English guides for "Logging in", "Resetting passwords", "Solving common problems" and "Raising support requests", "published in an easily accessible place, such as your intranet".
- "IT support staff should also receive regular training".
- Keep technical documentation supporting BCP/DR, and help maintain data protection, cyber and AUP policies.

**Other sources:**
- No numeric response or resolution times are given; the fetched text contains none.
- A secondary vendor article (15 Jul 2026) characterises the standard as focused on "outcomes and evidence rather than prescribing specific service desk structures or response times" — [Euroland IT Services](https://www.eurolanditservices.co.uk/dfe-it-support-standards-schools-2026/)
- DfE's Get Help Buying for Schools lists an "ICT Managed services & consultancy" buying option (Procurement Services) for "end-to-end ... ICT support, from day-to-day managed services and on-site assistance to specialist consultancy" — [GHBS solutions](https://get-help-buying-for-schools.education.gov.uk/solutions)

### Inferences
- EduHelpdesk features this standard directly validates:
  - mandatory ticket fields (description, timestamp, requester, approver, priority, action log)
  - requester status visibility (the existing /Portal)
  - recorded SLA targets with teaching-impact priority (existing SLA work days/periods)
  - escalation rules
  - logging of phone/email/in-person requests
  - published operating hours and emergency cover
  - a self-service knowledge base
  - the ability to "allow staff to reset their own passwords"
- The highest-value report is a one-click "Annual IT support review" pack for governors. Contents:
  - volumes by month, with start-of-year and exam-period peaks
  - top categories and systems by volume ("technologies or systems causing a high volume of requests")
  - SLA attainment
  - recurring problems
  - assets due for replacement
  - training needs
  - a record of IT support services/contracts
  - space for the written summary
- The "approver if required" field implies an approval step on some request types, e.g. purchases and admin privilege changes. This links to the cyber standard's SLT approval of admin-access changes.
- The standard discourages instant messages as a channel. A Teams integration should therefore turn messages into tracked tickets rather than replace them; EduHelpdesk's Teams webhook is currently outbound only.

### Gaps
- No DfE numeric SLA benchmarks or recommended helpdesk KPIs were found.
- No DfE guidance was found on in-house versus outsourced staffing ratios. The standard only says to consider a "hybrid approach" and to review contracts and value for money.

## 5. DfE / RPA / NCSC guidance on cyber incident response, staff cyber training, RPA cyber conditions and Police CyberAlarm that a helpdesk could track

### Takeaway
RPA cyber cover has four conditions: offline backups, NCSC (or equivalent) cyber training for staff and governors with IT access, registration with Police CyberAlarm, and a cyber response plan. The DfE cyber standard adds termly backup-test logs, an internal incident/near-miss log and a multi-body reporting checklist. DfE's new Cyber Security Hub (beta, 2026) supplies the response plan template, roles and incident processes. NCSC supplies the free staff training with a printable certificate (usable as completion evidence) and other free tools. Cyber Essentials v3.3 (April 2026) tightened MFA and patching, and DfE folded its "Cyber Essentials 2026" requirements into the cyber standard on 24 June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **RPA conditions.** The DfE Cyber Security Hub RPA page lists eligibility as: "be a public sector school", offline backups, "NCSC cyber security training", registering with "Police CyberAlarm", and "a cyber response plan". Page last reviewed 16 July 2026 — [DfE Cyber Security Hub: RPA](https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/risk-protection-arrangement)
- **RPA detail (secondary).** The four conditions are "have offline backups", "staff or governors with access to the school's information technology system must undertake cyber security training", "register with Police CyberAlarm" and "have a cyber response plan in place", with training described as "NCSC Cyber Security Training annually" — [RM blog, Sep 2025](https://www.rm.com/technology/blog/2025/september/understanding-cyber-security-in-the-risk-protection-arrangement-for-schools)
- **RPA rules PDF.** The September 2026 Academy membership rules exist as a gov.uk PDF — [RPA membership rules (academies) Sep 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf). A search-engine summary of it said cyber cover is £250,000 per loss and per membership year (£750,000 aggregate for a group network). It also said training is annual for "all staff and governors with access", "including supply staff if you allow them to use a supply login", and that at least one backup must be "entirely offline". I could NOT parse the PDF to verify this wording; treat it as unverified.
- **Cyber standard's RPA links.** RPA members "must evidence that the relevant users have undertaken the free ... NCSC training". The cyber response plan "is also a condition of cover if you have risk protection arrangement (RPA) cover". The backup level is "currently a condition of cover" — [GOV.UK cyber security](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-core-standard)
- **NCSC staff training.** A scripted presentation pack for INSET/staff meetings, or a self-learn video. "Once you have completed the training you can download a certificate to print". Published 21 April 2021, with page metadata modified 25 Aug 2026 — [NCSC cyber security training for school staff](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/information/cyber-security-training-schools)

  - Source: [NCSC cyber security for schools](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/section/education-skills/cyber-security-schools)
- **DfE Cyber Security Hub (beta)**, https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/ — [DfE Cyber Security Hub](https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/); [response plan page](https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/create-your-cyber-response-plan)

### Inferences
- An "RPA cyber readiness" panel in EduHelpdesk could track the four conditions with evidence:
  - date of last offline/immutable backup test (and log)
  - percentage of staff and governors with a current NCSC certificate (uploaded or date-recorded per person, expiring after 12 months; include supply logins if the school issues them)
  - Police CyberAlarm registration date/reference
  - cyber response plan document plus last-tested date
- Each item maps to a cited DfE or RPA requirement, so the panel is low-risk to build and easy to justify to SLT. Insurer evidence requests after an incident are a concrete use case.
- The cyber incident ticket type should hold:

- Exercise in a Box sessions and annual DR tests can be logged as scheduled compliance tasks with outcomes and attachments.

## 6. Recent (2024-2026) DfE updates, consultations and AI-related guidance affecting school IT support

### Takeaway
2024-2026 changes relevant to IT support:
- new standards: leadership/governance and laptops (Jan 2024), accessibility (Jan 2024), IT support (Nov 2025)
- Wi-Fi 7 as the upgrade minimum (Apr 2026)
- Cyber Essentials 2026 alignment of the cyber standard (Jun 2026)
- generative-AI risk assessment in filtering and monitoring (Jun and Aug 2026)
- the DfE Cyber Security Hub (2026)
- KCSIE 2026 (in force 1 Sep 2026), which ties cyber security and filtering/monitoring record-keeping into statutory safeguarding guidance
- DfE's Generative AI product safety standards (Jan 2025, upgraded Jan 2026)

### Cited Findings
- **Filtering & monitoring and AI.** 10 Jun 2026: "Updated for clarity on assessing the risk around generative AI". 25 Aug 2026: referenced "generative AI product safety standards". New review triggers include "new devices or generative-AI tools" — [content API](https://www.gov.uk/api/content/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard); [Smoothwall, 11 Jun 2026](https://smoothwall.com/resources/2026-dfe-filtering-and-monitoring-standards) (secondary)

- **KCSIE 2026 (in force 1 September 2026)** — [UK Safer Internet Centre, 14 Jul 2026](https://saferinternet.org.uk/blog/important-updates-to-keeping-children-safe-in-education-2026) (secondary); [SWGfL](https://swgfl.org.uk/magazine/keeping-children-safe-in-education-2026-what-do-schools-need-to-know/); [LGfL](https://safeblog.lgfl.net/kcsie-2026-filtering-monitoring-annual-review)

### Inferences
- Under the F&M standard and KCSIE 2026, new generative AI tools are a review trigger. An "approved apps / AI tools" register (DPIA done, product-safety-standards check, approver, date) in the contracts/software area would evidence several requirements at once:
  - the cyber standard's application approval
  - the contracts register's "List of approved apps"
  - the F&M trigger
- Because KCSIE 2026 makes record-keeping of filtering checks a safeguarding (governor-visible) matter, a "filtering check log" feature has stronger justification since September 2026 than before.
- Suggested priority for EduHelpdesk, by weight of citations, immediacy ("already"/"now") and fit with existing features:
  1. Asset register completeness: end-of-support date, separate age, disposal certificates, termly review sign-off.

  4. Leavers and movers (offboarding) workflow with termly account review.

  6. Contracts/licence register with expiry alerts and an approved-apps/AI register.
  7. Privileged/admin account register with SLT approval.
  8. Training/AUP and RPA readiness tracker.
  9. Knowledge base/self-service guides.
  10. Patch SLA tracking for 14-day CVSS ≥7.0 fixes (often better handled by RMM/patch tools, which the standard itself suggests: "a supported third-party patch management tool").
