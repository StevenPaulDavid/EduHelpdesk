# Legal, financial and safeguarding obligations on English schools and academy trusts that shape what an IT helpdesk / asset register must do or hold

Research date: 2026-10-03. England only. Editions used: KCSIE 2026 (in force 1 Sept 2026), Academy Trust Handbook 2026 (effective 1 Oct 2026), RPA academy membership rules Sept 2026, DfE "Data protection in schools" (updated 9 July 2026), DfE digital and technology standards pages (updated 16 Sept 2026). The DfE digital and technology standards themselves are covered by another researcher; they are cited here only where another obligation points to them. Quotes from KCSIE 2026, ATH 2026, the RPA rules, IRMS 2019 and the Hertfordshire 2024 schedule were checked against text pulled from the source documents. Other quotes come from page extracts.

## 1. UK GDPR / DPA 2018 / Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 as applied to schools: what a helpdesk holding staff and pupil data must support

### Takeaway
A helpdesk whose tickets, loans and onboarding tasks contain staff and pupil personal data falls under every part of the school's data protection duties: a ROPA entry with a retention period per data category, an annual personal-data audit, a log of all breaches (with ICO notice within 72 hours where there is risk), one-month SARs that must search "databases" and "record systems", DPIAs where children's data is processed at risk, and, since 19 June 2026, a data protection complaints route with acknowledgement within 30 days. The capabilities that would help most are:
- search and export of everything held about one person
- purge or anonymisation driven by retention rules, with a destruction log
- a breach register
- access audit logs and restricted categories

### Cited Findings
- DfE's "Data protection in schools" guidance was last updated **9 July 2026**. Its sub-pages include "The Data Use and Access Act 2025", "Dealing with subject access requests (SARs)", "Record keeping and management", "Managing breaches of data" and "Procuring educational technology (EdTech)". — [DfE: Data protection in schools](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools)
- **ROPA contents** must include "the categories of personal data you process", "the schedule for retaining each category of personal data", the DPO's name and contact details, and "a general description of your technical and organisational security measures". — [DfE: Data protection policies and procedures](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/data-protection-policies-and-procedures)
- **DPIA**: required "whenever the processing of personal data is likely to result in a 'high risk to the rights and freedoms' of individuals". DfE flags "vulnerable data subjects, including children (because of their age)", and says the DPIA should be "kept under regular review". — [DfE: policies and procedures](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/data-protection-policies-and-procedures)
- **Processors**: schools must ensure "there's an up-to-date controller-processor contract in place, if applicable". — [DfE: policies and procedures](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/data-protection-policies-and-procedures)
- **Monitoring data**: "where filtering and monitoring systems are used, schools should restrict access to monitoring data to authorised staff only". — [DfE: policies and procedures](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/data-protection-policies-and-procedures)
- **Annual audit**: "You should carry out an audit of all the personal data you hold each year to check it is up to date and still needed", and share the results with leaders, governors and trustees. — [DfE: Record keeping and management](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management)
- **Retention examples** from DfE:
  - secondary pupil records "Until the pupil's 25th birthday"
  - child protection files until the 25th birthday, or the 75th birthday where the file relates to child sexual abuse
  - finance and contract records 6 years
  - staff personnel files "6 years from termination of employment"
  - Source: [DfE: Record keeping and management](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management)
- **Secure disposal and destruction log**:
  - "Destroy storage media and hard disks to particles no larger than 6mm"
  - external companies must "provide a certificate of destruction"
  - "The Freedom of Information Act 2000 requires you to maintain a list of records that have been destroyed and who authorised their destruction."
  - Source: [DfE: Record keeping and management](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management)
- **SAR timescales**:
  - "A full SAR response must be sent to the requester within one calendar month"
  - this can be extended by up to two further months if the request is complex
  - "Education settings cannot extend a SAR response because it is the school holidays"
  - the clock can pause while "waiting for a requester to confirm their identification" or "seeking reasonable clarification"
  - Source: [DfE: SARs](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/dealing-with-subject-access-requests-sars)
- **SAR search scope**: schools must search "all records, including: emails (including those in deleted or trash folders), documents, spreadsheets, databases, record systems, CCTV…". The SAR log should hold "information about which records and systems were searched and what was found". Third parties should be redacted using tools where "redactions cannot be undone". — [DfE: SARs](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/dealing-with-subject-access-requests-sars)
- **DUAA 2025 changes**:
  - organisations "only have to make reasonable and proportionate searches" for SARs
  - organisations must "acknowledge complaints within 30 days"
  - ICO describes all data protection provisions as in force by **19 June 2026**
  - Source: [ICO: DUAA – what it means for organisations](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-what-does-it-mean-for-organisations/)
  - DfE's DUAA page says only that schools need "clear, accessible processes for individuals to raise concerns". — [DfE: DUAA 2025](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025)
- **Complaints detail** (secondary sources, seen as search summaries only):
  - there is no fixed deadline for the substantive response, which must be given "without undue delay"
  - an automated acknowledgement email can satisfy the 30-day acknowledgement
  - Sources: [Michelmores](https://www.michelmores.com/commercial-litigation-insight/ico-guidance-explained-the-new-data-protection-complaints-regime-from-june-2026/); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/get-ready-for-the-new-data-protection-complaints-handling-rules/)
- **Breach law**:
  - UK GDPR Art 33(1): notify the ICO "without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it"
  - Art 33(5): "The controller shall document any personal data breaches, comprising the facts relating to the personal data breach, its effects and the remedial action taken." This applies to all breaches, reported or not.
  - Source: [legislation.gov.uk UK GDPR Art 33](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)
- **DfE breach guidance**:
  - "it is good practice to record and investigate every personal data breach, however small"
  - record "what happened", "how it happened", "why it happened" and what prevents recurrence
  - the DPO can "spot any trends"
  - worked examples include a stolen tablet "with strong encryption and remote wipe" and ransomware
  - serious attacks should be reported to the NCSC
  - Source: [DfE: Managing breaches of data](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/managing-breaches-of-data)
- **IRMS on breaches and access**: "A log of breaches should be maintained and regularly reviewed". Schools need "to be able to maintain audit trails of who has access to information". — [IRMS Toolkit for Schools 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
- **EdTech, hosting and international transfers**. Schools should:
  - confirm "where and how data is stored, including any international transfers if the data is held or processed outside of the UK"
  - ask about "encryption, secure authentication, audit logging, intrusion detection"
  - make sure contracts cover "security measures, sub-processors, deletion and return of data, breach notification, and staff access control"
  - agree timescales for "data transfer and deletion, including how backups and archives will be handled"
  - Source: [DfE: Procuring EdTech](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/procuring-educational-technology-edtech)

### Inferences
- **Person search and SAR/FOI export.** One search for a person should cover requester, affected user, pupil or staff names in ticket text, comments, attachments, loans, onboarding tasks, portal submissions, project and quote contacts, and the audit log. The output should be an exportable bundle plus an auto-generated list of "systems/tables searched". This maps directly to DfE's SAR record-keeping expectation.
- **Retention engine.** Retention should be configurable per category (ticket category, loan, onboarding checklist, asset record, attachments), with scheduled anonymise-or-delete and a legal-hold flag. Each run should write to a destruction register: record series, count, date, and who authorised it. This is the FOIA list that DfE and IRMS cite. Purges must also cover backups, or the retention policy must say how long backups keep data.
- **Breach register module.** Fields:
  - facts, effects and remedial action (the Art 33(5) minimum)
  - date/time of awareness, with a 72-hour countdown
  - whether reported to the ICO, with the reason if not
  - whether data subjects were informed
  - DPO involvement
  - links to tickets and assets
- **Lost-device link.** Setting an asset to "Lost/Stolen" should prompt a breach assessment and record encryption status and remote-wipe evidence. DfE's stolen-tablet example shows that this evidence is what makes a breach low-risk.
- **Access control and audit.** Role-restricted sensitive categories (HR, safeguarding-adjacent, disciplinary) and a "viewed by" audit log support the IRMS audit-trail point and the DfE expectation of restricted monitoring data.
- **DPIA/ROPA support pack.** An admin page or PDF listing the personal-data fields EduHelpdesk stores, the retention defaults, the hosting location (self-hosted UK; or the Azure UK region if moved), sub-processors (none when self-hosted), and security measures (2FA, logs). Schools need this to complete their own DPIA and ROPA entry. A vendor-neutral, self-hosted app answers DfE's EdTech "where is data stored" question cleanly.
- **New request types with their own clocks:**
  - "Data protection complaint", with a 30-day acknowledgement deadline and auto-acknowledgement
  - "SAR", one calendar month, with stop-the-clock
  - "FOI", 20 school days (see section 9)

  EduHelpdesk's existing SLA engine counts weekends only and has no term dates, so it cannot compute "school days" yet.

### Gaps
- The exact commencement date of the DUAA "reasonable and proportionate" SAR search provision was not confirmed from a primary source. A search summary claimed 19 June 2025 with retrospective effect from 1 January 2024. The ICO page describes a phased June 2025 to June 2026 rollout.
- No DfE or ICO guidance names a retention period for IT helpdesk tickets. Schools must set their own (see section 2).
- Whether an internal helpdesk counts as "high risk" processing that needs a DPIA is a school judgement. No source addresses helpdesks specifically.

## 2. Records retention: IRMS Information Management Toolkit for Schools / Academies, applied to IT records

### Takeaway
The IRMS schedule has no "helpdesk ticket" class. Neighbouring classes give defensible defaults:

| Record class | Retention |
|---|---|
| Inventories of furniture and equipment | Current year + 6 years |
| Burglary, theft and vandalism reports | Current year + 6 years |
| Invoices, orders, requisitions | Current financial year + 6 years |
| Contracts under signature | Last payment + 6 years |
| Contracts under seal | Last payment + 12 years |
| Electronic visitor management systems | Last entry + 6 years |

IRMS also requires deletion to be a managed, authorised and auditable process that reaches backups, with destruction documented.

### Cited Findings
- From the IRMS Schools Toolkit 2019, Asset Management:
  - 2.5.2 "Inventories of furniture and equipment": "Current year + 6 years", SECURE DISPOSAL
  - 2.5.3 "Burglary, theft and vandalism report forms": "Current year + 6 years"
  - Source: [IRMS Toolkit for Schools 2019 (PDF)](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
  - The same rows appear in a Hertfordshire maintained-schools schedule (Summer 2024) based on IRMS. — [Herts Records Retention Schedule (Maintained Schools) Summer 2024](https://www.howedell.herts.sch.uk/attachments/download.asp?file=244&type=pdf)
- Further rows from the Hertfordshire 2024 schedule ([source](https://www.howedell.herts.sch.uk/attachments/download.asp?file=244&type=pdf)):
  - 2.5.7 "Invoices, receipts, order books and requisitions, delivery notices": "Current financial year + 6 years"
  - 2.5.12 contracts under seal: "Last payment on the contract + 12 years"
  - 2.5.13 contracts under signature: "Last payment on the contract + 6 years" (Limitation Act 1980)
  - 2.5.14 contract monitoring: "Life of contract + 6 or 12 years"
  - 2.2.7 "Visitor management systems (including electronic systems, visitors books and signing-in sheets)": "Last entry in the visitors' book + 6 years"
  - 2.5.5 "Loans and grants managed by the school": "Date of last payment on the loan + 12 years then review". This is a financial loans class, not device loans.
- **Deletion as a process**:
  - "Deletion of electronic records should be a managed and auditable process"
  - deletions "should be authorised by the relevant senior officer"
  - before deleting, check there is "no related litigation or investigation"
  - "Processes must be in place to ensure that all backups and copies are included in the deletion process"
  - data that cannot be fully deleted should be "put beyond use"
  - Source: [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
- **Documenting destruction**:
  - "it is vital to keep a record of all archiving, destruction, deletion and digitisation"
  - IRMS warns that schools "must assess whether they are creating another piece of Personal Identifiable Information (PII) by maintaining a record of evidence"
  - certificates of destruction "should be retained with details of individual records destroyed"
  - Source: [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
- **Information assets**: schools should produce "both an Information Asset Register (IAR) and a Record of Processing Activity (RoPA)". Information assets are "usually systems in which data is held". An Information Asset Owner should be identified "for each asset or group of assets". — [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
- **Child protection material**: should be stored "in a separate area of the record or keep in a separate linked file so as to limit access to specific staff". — [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)
- **Retention schedule duty**: under the FOIA, "schools should maintain a Retention Schedule listing the series of records which the school creates or maintains". — [Herts 2024 schedule](https://www.howedell.herts.sch.uk/attachments/download.asp?file=244&type=pdf)
- **Toolkit access**: the IRMS Schools Toolkit is reportedly now online-only and members-only. This comes from a search-result summary and was not verified on the IRMS site. — [IRMS Schools Toolkit](https://irms.org.uk/page/SchoolsToolkit)

### Inferences
- Suggested default retention presets EduHelpdesk could ship, each editable and labelled with its IRMS anchor:

| Record | Suggested default | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Asset records and their disposal evidence | Disposal year + 6 | Inventories 2.5.2 |
| Loss/theft tickets and loss register | Current year + 6 | 2.5.3 |
| Purchase orders, quotes, invoices attached to projects | Financial year + 6 | 2.5.7 |
| Supplier contracts and renewals | Last payment + 6 (or + 12 if sealed) | 2.5.12 / 2.5.13 |
| Onboarding checklists containing HR data | No longer than the personnel file (6 years after leaving); operational tasks much shorter | DfE personnel file period |
| Routine IT fault tickets | Short, school-defined (e.g. current year + 1 to 2) | No authority; a school decision |
| Device loan agreements | Return date + 6 | Inventory/contract classes (inference) |

- Retention clocks need anchor dates the app does not hold today: asset disposal date, loan return date, staff leaving date, contract last-payment date, and pupil date of birth (for the 25th-birthday rule).
- The destruction register should log record series and counts, not names, to follow the IRMS warning about creating new PII.

### Gaps
- The current online IRMS edition (post-2019) could not be accessed (members-only). The schedules used are the 2019 edition and a 2024 local-authority derivative.
- No verified IRMS retention period was found for CCTV, door-access or system/security logs.
- No IRMS class exists specifically for helpdesk tickets or device loan agreements.

## 3. Safeguarding: KCSIE 2026 filtering and monitoring, escalation to the DSL, and what not to hold in a general helpdesk

### Takeaway
KCSIE 2026 (in force 1 Sept 2026) makes four points that matter here:
- The filtering and monitoring review must happen "at least once every academic year".
- The review is run by the SLT lead "with the support of the school's designated safeguarding lead and IT support", must check filtering "on all internet connected devices in all relevant locations", and "a record should be kept of these checks".
- All staff, IT included, must act immediately on concerns and speak to the DSL.
- Safeguarding records are confidential, stored securely and kept in a separate child protection file.

So the helpdesk should evidence filtering and monitoring checks and route concerns out. It should not store safeguarding details itself.

### Cited Findings
- KCSIE 2026 came into force on 1 September 2026, replacing KCSIE 2025 (in force 1 Sept 2025). — [DfE: KCSIE collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/keeping-children-safe-in-education--2)
- **Paras 173–175, the annual review**: governing bodies "should ensure their school or college has appropriate filtering and monitoring systems in place and ensure that a review of their effectiveness is carried out at least once every academic year. Reviews should be carried out by the SLT member responsible for filtering and monitoring, with the support of the school's designated safeguarding lead and IT support. They should include checks that filtering is working appropriately on all internet connected devices in all relevant locations, and a record should be kept of these checks." The same passage says leadership and relevant staff should "know how to escalate concerns when identified". — [KCSIE 2026 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **The DfE filtering and monitoring standards** (as summarised in KCSIE) say schools should:
  - "identify and assign roles and responsibilities to manage filtering and monitoring systems"
  - "review filtering and monitoring provision at least annually"
  - "block harmful and inappropriate content without unreasonably impacting teaching and learning"
  - "have effective monitoring strategies in place"
  - Source: [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Para 176**: schools can use DfE's "Plan technology for your school" service to self-assess against the filtering and monitoring standards. **Para 177**: governing bodies "should review the standards and discuss with IT staff and service providers what more needs to be done". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Para 163**: governing bodies "should be careful that 'over blocking' does not lead to unreasonable restrictions as to what children can be taught". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Para 169**: the online safety policy should include "appropriate filtering and monitoring on school devices and school networks". This is relevant to devices loaned for home use. — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Paras 178–179, "Information security and access management"**: governing bodies should protect personal information and ensure "appropriate cyber security systems". "This should be approached as part of the school or college's wider safeguarding responsibilities", with reference to the DfE Cyber security standards. — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **DSL role**: the DSL takes lead responsibility "including online safety and understanding the filtering and monitoring systems and processes in place". **Para 153**: all-staff training includes "the expectations, applicable roles and responsibilities in relation to filtering and monitoring". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Paras 58–59, escalation**: "If staff have any concerns about a child's welfare, they should act on them immediately." Staff "should follow their own organisation's child protection policy and speak to the designated safeguarding lead (or a deputy)". "Data protection laws do not prevent the sharing of information for the purposes of keeping children safe". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Para 74, record keeping**:
  - "All concerns, discussions and decisions made, and the reasons for those decisions, should be recorded in writing."
  - "Information should be kept confidential and stored securely. It is good practice to keep concerns and referrals in a separate child protection file for each child."
  - Source: [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Paras 520–521, low-level concerns about adults**: "All low-level concerns should be recorded in writing". Records "must be kept confidential, held securely and comply with data protection laws". This is relevant where IT staff spot staff misuse. — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **Annex B**: the DSL should liaise with staff "(especially teachers, pastoral support staff, school nurses, IT technicians…)". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **SWGfL (9 July 2026)** reads the 2026 change as moving annual review from "encouraged" to explicit. It says records should cover when checks were done, which devices and environments were tested, issues found and remedial action. This is a secondary interpretation. — [SWGfL](https://swgfl.org.uk/magazine/keeping-children-safe-in-education-2026-what-do-schools-need-to-know/)
- **Conflicting secondary source**: safeguard-hub.org places the filtering review at "Paragraph 168" with "governors formally accountable", and also places mobile phones at para 168. The primary text puts the filtering review in paras 173–175 and mobile phones in para 170, and does not contain that "formally accountable" wording. Treat safeguard-hub's paragraph references as unreliable. — [safeguard-hub](https://safeguard-hub.org/articles/kcsie-2026-changes/) vs [KCSIE 2026 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Filtering and monitoring check record.** A recurring compliance task (at least once per academic year, optionally termly) with a checklist. Rows are generated from the asset register by device type, site and network (staff and pupil Wi-Fi, guest network, loaned devices off-site). Each row records the date, tester, result, issue and remedial action, with sign-off fields for the SLT lead and DSL. The output is a PDF for governors and Ofsted. This produces exactly the record para 173 asks for, and the asset register supplies "all internet connected devices in all relevant locations".
- **Filter change log.** Unblock and block requests submitted as tickets should record who approved them, which can be the DSL for category changes. This evidences the "over blocking" balance in para 163 and gives an audit trail of filter changes.
- **"Safeguarding concern?" escape route.** On the portal and on ticket forms, this route shows the school's DSL contact and its CPOMS/MyConcern route and tells the user not to type details. Any ticket marked this way should hold only a minimal stub ("passed to DSL, date, by whom"), not the content. This follows para 74 (separate, secure child protection file) and IRMS ("limit access to specific staff").
- **No harmful content in tickets.** Provide a restricted "Sensitive / safeguarding-adjacent" category visible only to named roles and excluded from search, reports and exports. Add a policy prompt against attaching screenshots of harmful content. Monitoring-system alerts should not be piped into the general queue, in line with DfE: "restrict access to monitoring data to authorised staff only".
- **Staff misuse.** Concerns about adults found by IT (low-level concerns) should route to the headteacher or DSL outside the helpdesk, or into a restricted category.
- **Loaned-device check.** The loan checklist should include a "filtering agent installed/verified" step for loaned devices (para 169, "school devices").

### Gaps
- No primary source prescribes the format or content of the filtering and monitoring check record. SWGfL's field list is interpretation.
- No guidance names "IT helpdesk systems" as places where safeguarding information must not be held. The "do not store" recommendation is inferred from para 74 and IRMS.

## 4. Finance and assets: Academy Trust Handbook 2026, SFVS, schemes for financing schools, RPA, procurement and related parties

### Takeaway
**Academy trusts** (ATH 2026, effective 1 Oct 2026) must:
- "maintain a fixed asset register"
- notify DfE of fraud, theft or irregularity over £5,000, individually or cumulatively in a financial year, with dates, value, recurrence measures, police referral and insurance/RPA offset
- get DfE approval for write-offs beyond delegated limits
- never pay ransoms
- run a competitive procurement procedure and record decisions
- report every related-party contract in advance, with DfE approval above £40,000

**Maintained schools** must keep an inventory of moveable non-capital assets (statutory scheme guidance), and SFVS Q8 expects "an up-to-date asset register and adequate insurance".

**RPA cover** excludes "unexplained" disappearance or inventory shortage, and claims need "books, records and documents". A good asset register is therefore direct claim evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **ATH 2026** is effective from 1 October 2026. Recent updates "clarified procurement requirements for energy and management information systems (MIS)" at paras 2.29–2.30. — [DfE: Academy trust handbook](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/academy-trust-handbook)
- **ATH control framework** bullets include:
  - "manage and oversee assets and maintain a fixed asset register"
  - "ensure a risk register is maintained and reviewed by the board"
  - "reduce the risk of fraud and theft"
  - Source: [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Procurement (ATH 2.25)**: trusts must ensure:
  - "a competitive procurement procedure is in place and incorporated into the trust's financial framework"
  - "procurement rules and thresholds in the Procurement Act 2023 and its associated regulations are followed"
  - "Find a Tender service is used"
  - "appropriate due diligence is in place"
  - Source: [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **ATH 2.27**: "Trusts must consider DfE opportunities when making purchasing decisions for goods and services and record their decision-making." **ATH 2.30**: MIS contracts must be awarded under DfE's MIS framework once it launches (planned September 2027). — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Write-offs (ATH 5.19–5.20)**: DfE prior approval is needed for "writing-off debts and losses" beyond delegated limits. The limits are:
  - "1% of total annual income or £45,000 (whichever is smaller) per single transaction"
  - cumulatively 2.5% of income (max £250,000) per category per year for trusts without two years of timely unqualified accounts
  - 5% (max £250,000) for other trusts
  - Source: [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
  - Trusts should write off losses only "after careful appraisal, including whether all reasonable recovery action has been taken" (para 5.21, from a page extract). — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Disposals (ATH 5.23–5.24)**: DfE approval is only needed for land, buildings and heritage assets. "Other than land, buildings and heritage assets, trusts can dispose of other fixed assets without DfE's approval, subject to achieving the best price that can reasonably be obtained, and maintaining the principles of regularity, propriety and value for money." — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Fraud and theft reporting (ATH 6.10)**: "The board of trustees must notify DfE as soon as possible of all instances of fraud, theft or irregularity exceeding £5,000 individually, or £5,000 cumulatively in any financial year." The notification must include:
  - "full details of the events with dates"
  - "the financial value of the loss"
  - "measures taken to prevent recurrence"
  - whether the matter was referred to the police
  - "whether insurance or the RPA have offset any loss"
  - Source: [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Cybercrime and ransoms (ATH 6.14–6.15)**: trusts "must also be aware of the risk of cybercrime, put in place proportionate controls and take appropriate action where a cyber security incident has occurred". "Trusts must not pay any ransom or extortion demands, including cyber ransomware." — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026). The ban began in ATH 2025; ATH 2024 had required consent instead. — [Stone King, 2 Sept 2025](https://www.stoneking.co.uk/literature/e-bulletins/overview-academy-trust-handbook-2025-whats-new-and-what-academy-trust)
- **Related parties (ATH 5.42–5.43)**: trusts "must report all contracts and other agreements with related parties to DfE in advance". DfE's prior approval is needed where a related-party supply contract "exceeds £40,000 in the same financial year ending 31 August". — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **Insurance and scrutiny**: trusts must have adequate insurance or RPA membership, and "must cooperate with risk management auditors" (2.45–2.47). Internal scrutiny covers "financial and non-financial controls" (3.2). Trusts "should be working towards" the DfE digital and technology standards by 2030 (1.21). — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **SFVS (maintained schools)**: the current edition on gov.uk is 2025 to 2026, due 31 March 2026. — [DfE: SFVS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/schools-financial-value-standard). Relevant checklist questions:
  - Q8 asks about a business continuity or disaster recovery plan, which should include "an up-to-date asset register and adequate insurance", with asset records "for insurance purposes, to be kept where it would not be vulnerable to disaster"
  - Q27: "adequate arrangements in place to guard against fraud and theft by staff, contractors and suppliers"
  - Q20–23: procurement, value for money and "DfE approved frameworks"
  - Q4: business interests registered
  - Q26: "a complete list of related party transactions"
  - Source: [SFVS checklist guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/schools-financial-value-standard/schools-financial-value-standard-sfvs-checklist-guidance)
- **Schemes for financing schools (maintained)**. The DfE statutory guidance, as reproduced in local authority schemes, says:
  - the scheme must require each school "to maintain an inventory of its moveable non-capital assets", with "basic authorisation procedures for disposal of assets"
  - schools are free to set their own arrangements for assets "worth less than £1,000"
  - schemes should encourage schools "to register anything that is portable and attractive, such as a camera"
  - Source: search-result summaries of local authority schemes; DfE's own page returned 404. — [Bolton Scheme for Financing Schools April 2026 (statutory guidance appendix)](https://bolton.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s13162/6.3.26%20Scheme%20for%20Financing%20Schools%20April%202026-Appendix.pdf); [Brent Scheme 2026-27](https://democracy.brent.gov.uk/documents/s159592/Scheme%20for%20Financing%20Schools%202026-27.pdf)
  - Local variants (e.g. "all items with a value of over £200 or which are deemed portable and attractive"; headteacher can approve disposals under £500, governing body above) appear in some local authority schemes. These are local, not national. — [search summary of LA schemes](https://www.york.gov.uk/downloads/file/9569/scheme-for-financing-schools-2025)
- **RPA academy membership rules (Sept 2026)**:
  - **Exclusions**: the material damage exclusions cover "disappearance, unexplained or inventory shortage, misfiling or misplacing of information".
  - **Temporary removal**: cover applies "up to a limit of £20,000 any one loss whilst temporarily removed from the Premises for provision of education, lesson preparation…".
  - **Claims evidence**: claims require "any evidence and information, books, records and documents required to assess the claim".
  - **Unattended vehicles**: an "Unattended Vehicle" is defined, and money/property exclusions apply to unattended vehicles.
  - Source: [RPA membership rules – academy trusts, Sept 2026 (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)
- **RPA and lent laptops**: an RPA FAQ (January 2023) reportedly says laptops lent to staff or pupils for home use are covered only where the user's own insurance does not cover them. This was seen in a search summary only and not verified for 2026. — [RPA FAQs Jan 2023](https://www.rpaclaimforms.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/RPA-FAQs-Update-January-2023-Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Asset register fields auditors and insurers need**:
  - cost and acquisition date
  - supplier and invoice/PO reference
  - funding source (capital, grant, devolved formula capital)
  - "capitalised" flag (threshold configurable per trust)
  - "portable and attractive" flag
  - location, custodian and serial number
  - status history
  - disposal date, method, proceeds/best-price evidence and approver
- **Annual stock check.** Run per site with scan or tick-off, producing a variance report. Unaccounted items move to a write-off workflow that checks the ATH delegated limits and records recovery action taken.
- **Loss and theft register.** Fields: dates, value, police crime reference, recurrence measures, and RPA claim/offset. Keep a running per-financial-year total that warns when cumulative fraud/theft passes £5,000, which triggers the ATH 6.10 DfE notification. This register also supports SFVS Q27 and RPA claims, and avoids the "unexplained disappearance" exclusion.
- **Off-premises view.** A list of devices currently off premises (loans, staff laptops), showing exposure against the RPA £20,000 temporary-removal limit.
- **Procurement evidence on projects/quotes.** EduHelpdesk already holds per-supplier quotes. Add:
  - "DfE framework / Buying for Schools considered?" with a reason (ATH 2.27 "record their decision-making"; SFVS Q23)
  - chosen-supplier rationale
  - a related-party flag on suppliers, with a warning as related-party spend nears £40,000 in an academy year (Sept–Aug)
  - a conflict-of-interest declaration
- **Off-site register copy.** A scheduled export of the register (PDF/CSV) to a location outside the main system, per SFVS Q8 "not vulnerable to disaster".
- **Two financial years.** Reporting must handle both academy (Sept–Aug) and maintained-school (Apr–Mar) years.

### Gaps
- Capitalisation thresholds for academies are set in each trust's accounting policy. The Academies Accounts Direction was not checked.
- DfE's own statutory guidance page on schemes for financing schools returned 404. Its wording here comes from local authority reproductions and search summaries.
- Procurement Act 2023 threshold values and any DfE quote thresholds were not verified. Quote thresholds below the statutory levels are set in each school's or trust's finance policy.
- The 2026 RPA position on loaned laptops was not verified beyond the temporary-removal clause.

## 5. Disposal and environment: WEEE, secure data destruction certificates, waste duty of care, sustainability

### Takeaway
The DfE device standard (updated 16 Sept 2026) says that at disposal IT support must meet WEEE and data protection requirements, hold "certificates for WEEE, disposal and destruction of data", and update the IT asset register. NCSC and DfE data protection guidance set the sanitisation and destruction standards (6mm particles, certified providers such as ADISA or CAS-S). The waste duty of care code requires waste transfer notes to be kept 2 years (hazardous consignment notes 3 years). A disposal workflow that holds these documents against each asset is direct compliance evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **DfE laptops, desktops and tablets standard (updated 16 Sept 2026)**. When disposing of devices, IT support must ensure:
  - "Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) regulations and data protection requirements are met"
  - "they have certificates for WEEE, disposal and destruction of data"
  - "the IT asset register is updated"
  - data is removed or destroyed to prevent security incidents

  The standard also expects schools to "Keep registers relating to hardware and systems up to date". Minimum support is "3 years" for tablets and "5 years" for laptops and desktops. — [DfE: Laptops, desktops and tablets standard](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/laptops-desktops-and-tablets)
- **DfE cyber security standards**: the asset register should record "digital technology end-of-support dates" (from a page extract). — [DfE: Cyber security standards](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-standards-for-schools-and-colleges)
- **NCSC secure sanitisation**:
  - "Record the life cycle of your ESM (that is, what is it being used to store, where, and for how long)"
  - use disposal companies holding "recognised certifications or work to recognised standards (such as CAS-S or ADISA Certification)"
  - on encrypted devices, a factory reset "deletes the encryption keys"
  - physical destruction to "particles of 6mm or less", with particle size verified
  - Source: [NCSC: Secure sanitisation of storage media](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/secure-sanitisation-storage-media)
- **DfE data protection guidance**: "Destroy storage media and hard disks to particles no larger than 6mm". External companies must "provide a certificate of destruction". — [DfE: Record keeping and management](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management)
- **Waste duty of care code**:
  - keep transfer notes "2 years for non-hazardous waste"
  - keep "three years for hazardous waste consignment notes"
  - check the receiver holds an environmental permit or "a valid registration as a carrier, broker or dealer of waste", using "the public register"
  - waste may need "particular treatment or handling, for example batteries or waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE)"
  - Source: [Defra: Waste duty of care code of practice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/waste-duty-of-care-code-of-practice/waste-duty-of-care-code-of-practice)
- **IRMS certificates**: certificates of destruction "should be retained with details of individual records destroyed". The chain of custody transfers legal responsibility to an approved contractor. — [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Disposal workflow on assets.** Status steps: Retire, then Awaiting disposal, Data sanitised (method: crypto-erase/overwrite/shred; verified by), Collected, Certificate received, Disposed. An asset cannot close without:
  - the data-destruction certificate number or file
  - the WEEE / waste transfer note, keeping its own 2-year (or 3-year hazardous) retention
  - carrier registration number
  - provider certification (ADISA/CAS-S)
  - disposal value or proceeds (ATH 5.24 "best price")
- **Batch disposals.** One certificate or transfer note should be attachable to many assets, since collections are batched.
- **Lifecycle reports.** An end-of-support date field with a report of devices out of support or due to expire (DfE cyber and device standards), plus redeploy/cascade history for sustainability reporting (reuse before recycle).

### Gaps
- Device-specific expectations in the DfE Sustainability and climate change strategy could not be verified. Search found only building-level commitments (net zero new builds, sustainable ICT in new builds), through secondary summaries. The digital and technology "sustainability" standard page URL returned 404 and is left to the standards researcher.
- The WEEE Regulations 2013 obligations on business (non-household) users were not checked at primary source.

## 6. Accessibility: Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018, WCAG 2.2 AA, Equality Act

### Takeaway
Schools are only partly covered by the 2018 Regulations: content must comply only where it relates to "essential online administrative functions". Intranets and extranets published before 23 September 2019 are exempt until substantially revised. GOV.UK now names WCAG 2.2 AA as the standard. An internal IT helpdesk is probably outside the strict duty, but a requester portal used by parents or pupils (for example device-loan requests) could arguably be "essential administrative". Building to WCAG 2.2 AA and offering an accessibility statement template is low-cost insurance and a selling point for any school adopting the app.

### Cited Findings
- **Regulation 4(2)**: the Regulations do not apply to the websites and apps of "schools or nurseries, except for the content of their websites or mobile applications relating to essential online administrative functions". They also exempt "content of extranets and intranets published before 23rd September 2019, until such websites undergo a substantial revision". — [legislation.gov.uk: SI 2018/952 reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952/regulation/4)
- **GOV.UK guidance**:
  - schools are partially exempt and must make accessible the content "people need in order to use their services, for example a form that lets you outline school meal preferences"
  - the required standard is WCAG 2.2 AA
  - bodies must "publish an accessibility statement"
  - a disproportionate-burden exemption exists
  - Source: [GOV.UK: Accessibility requirements for public sector websites and apps](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps)

### Inferences
- The requester portal should target WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operation, focus visibility, contrast in both themes, labelled form fields, error messages, and target size (a new 2.2 criterion). This matters most for the tile launcher and the "Report a problem" flow, which parents or pupils may use.
- Ship an editable "Accessibility statement" page for the portal so schools can publish one.
- The technician UI is staff-only. Equality Act reasonable adjustments for disabled staff argue for the same baseline. The Equality Act duties were not verified this session; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- No official interpretation was found of whether a school's staff IT helpdesk or requester portal counts as an "essential online administrative function".
- Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment (s.20) and public sector equality duty (s.149) applications to internal school systems were not fetched or verified in this session.

## 7. Device loans to pupils and staff: agreements, consent, liability, records

### Takeaway
No statute prescribes device-loan records. Practice evidence comes from templates, past DfE device programmes and insurance:
- a signed loan agreement / acceptable use policy for each device, recording device ID, serial number, condition, issue and return dates, signatures, liability for loss or damage, and a duty to report loss promptly (as a possible data breach)
- school devices used at home remain within KCSIE's "school devices" filtering expectation
- RPA cover off-premises is limited

### Cited Findings
- **Education Data Hub staff device loan agreement template** (Derbyshire County Council). It covers device identification, serial numbers and condition, dates issued and returned, signatures, user obligations, loss and damage liability, and data breach reporting when devices are lost. It aims for "clear expectations on conduct and security". — [Education Data Hub: Staff Device Loan Agreement](https://educationdatahub.org.uk/resources/device-loan-agreement/)
- **Example school acceptable use policy** (search summary only): staff mobile technology "remains the property of the school and is provided on a loaned basis", and users "must sign for it and bear responsibility for its care". — [Hollingwood School AUP 2024-25](https://www.hollingwood.derbyshire.sch.uk/_site/data/files/key-information/policies/2024-dec/acceptable-use-of-it-policy-for-staff-and-volunteers-24-25.pdf)
- **DfE pupil device programme (2020)**: devices "contained a paper loan agreement". The local authority asked schools to return outstanding forms "to ensure we have accurate records of the devices being distributed" (Bradford, 2 Oct 2020). — [Bradford Schools Online](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/Schools/News_View.aspx?Id=19514)
- **KCSIE 2026 para 169**: the online safety policy should include "appropriate filtering and monitoring on school devices and school networks". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **RPA**: cover for property "temporarily removed from the Premises for provision of education, lesson preparation…" is limited to £20,000 any one loss. — [RPA academy rules Sept 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)
- **DfE breach example**: a stolen tablet "with strong encryption and remote wipe". — [DfE: Managing breaches](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/managing-breaches-of-data)

### Inferences
- **Loan record** should hold:
  - the agreement version and the acceptable use policy version accepted
  - signature (e-signature on the portal or an uploaded scan)
  - for pupils, the parent/carer signatory
  - condition notes or photos at issue and at return
  - accessories and kit contents (EduHelpdesk loan kits already exist)
  - reason code
  - due date with reminders and overdue escalation
  - charges or damage outcome
- **Lost or stolen loan.** One action should open a loss/theft register entry (police reference, value, the £5,000 ATH aggregate) and a breach assessment (encryption and remote-wipe evidence).
- **Pre-issue checklist.** Include "filtering/monitoring agent active" and "encryption on" steps before issue, as evidence for KCSIE para 169 and for DfE breach-risk reasoning.
- **Retention.** Keep agreements to the return date + 6 years, by analogy with the inventory and contract classes. This is an inference, not an IRMS row.

### Gaps
- No DfE or ICO guidance was found on parental liability for damage, or on the lawful basis for pupil device loans (public task vs consent). Typical liability terms vary by school and come mostly from US sources, which were excluded.
- The 2026 RPA treatment of devices at staff or pupil homes was not verified beyond the temporary-removal clause.

## 8. Cyber: Cyber Essentials, RPA cyber cover conditions, incident reporting (Report Fraud / Action Fraud, NCSC, DfE), ATH

### Takeaway
- **Cyber Essentials** is not mandatory for schools or trusts. It is mandatory only for colleges, under their funding agreement.
- **RPA cyber cover** (Sept 2026 rules) needs four conditions, evidenced at claim time:
  - DfE 3-2-1 backups
  - annual NCSC training for all staff and governors with IT access, completed by the start of the membership year
  - Police CyberAlarm registration
  - a Cyber Response Plan
- **Reporting routes** (DfE cyber standards, 16 Sept 2026):
  - Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud)
  - the DfE sector cyber team at Sector.Incidentreporting@education.gov.uk
  - the RPA or the school's insurer
  - NCSC for major incidents
  - the ICO within 72 hours for high-risk data breaches
- **ATH 6.15** bans ransom payments. **KCSIE 2026** frames cyber security as safeguarding.

### Cited Findings
- **DfE cyber security standards (updated 16 Sept 2026)**. Report incidents to:
  - "Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040, or the Report Fraud website"
  - "the DfE sector cyber team at Sector.Incidentreporting@education.gov.uk"
  - "your RPA or cyber insurance provider"
  - "the NCSC website if the incident or attack causes long-term school closure"
  - "the ICO website within 72 hours, where a high-risk data breach has or may have occurred"
  - Source (page extract): [DfE: Cyber security standards](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-standards-for-schools-and-colleges)
- **Same page**: "Cyber Essentials is a requirement for colleges under their funding agreement. Some schools may wish to complete it as part of their cyber security activities." Incidents should be "recorded in an internal incident report or system" (page extract). — [DfE: Cyber security standards](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/cyber-security-standards-for-schools-and-colleges)
- **RPA academy rules Sept 2026, cyber conditions**. "In the event of a claim the Member will be required to evidence compliance with all of the conditions below":
  1. Meet the DfE backup standard: "at least 3 backup copies of important data, on at least 2 separate devices, at least 1 must be off-site".
  2. "All Employees or Governors who have access to the Member's information technology system must undertake NCSC training annually". The member must "evidence that any Employee or Governor involved in the claim has undertaken the NCSC training", with "NCSC training to be completed by the start of the Membership Year".
  3. "All Members must register with Police CyberAlarm".
  4. "All Members must have a Cyber Response Plan in place".

  The extract shows a member retention of the first £2,000 of each loss (£1,000 for primary academies) and a £750,000 group-network aggregate. — [RPA academy rules Sept 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a7c63be783bd442d7805caa/rpa-membership-rules-academy-september-2026.pdf)
- **RPA cyber limit and contact**: cover is limited to "£250,000 for any one loss and in any one membership year". The cyber incident contact is RPAresponse@CyberClan.com / 0800 368 6378. — [DfE: Make a claim through the RPA](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/make-a-claim-through-the-risk-protection-arrangement-rpa). The DfE Cyber Security Hub lists the same four conditions (page reviewed 16 July 2026). — [DfE Cyber Security Hub: RPA](https://cyber-security-hub.education.gov.uk/risk-protection-arrangement)
- **RM blog (Sept 2025)** gives the same four conditions and found no Cyber Essentials requirement in RPA. — [RM](https://www.rm.com/technology/blog/2025/september/understanding-cyber-security-in-the-risk-protection-arrangement-for-schools)
- **ATH 6.14–6.15**: trusts must "put in place proportionate controls and take appropriate action where a cyber security incident has occurred". "Trusts must not pay any ransom or extortion demands, including cyber ransomware." — [ATH 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2026-effective-from-1-october-2026)
- **KCSIE 2026 paras 178–179**: cyber security is to be "approached as part of the school or college's wider safeguarding responsibilities". — [KCSIE 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a9081309a177a1decf97b00/Keeping_children_safe_in_education_2026.pdf)
- **DfE breach guidance**: for serious attacks, "report the incident to the National Cyber Security Centre". — [DfE: Managing breaches](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/managing-breaches-of-data)

### Inferences
- **RPA evidence pack** that the helpdesk could hold:
  - an NCSC training register (person, date, membership year), fed from the onboarding checklist and an annual recurring task, with a report of staff or governors not yet trained before 1 September
  - a backup verification log: recurring "test restore / offline copy check" tasks with results
  - the Police CyberAlarm registration record
  - a versioned Cyber Response Plan document with a review date
- **Cyber incident ticket type** with a reporting checklist and timestamps:
  - Report Fraud reference
  - DfE sector email sent
  - RPA/CyberClan notified
  - NCSC (if major)
  - ICO decision against the 72-hour clock
  - a "no ransom" reminder for trusts
  - an incident timeline for the internal incident record the DfE standard asks for
- **Asset register reports.** End-of-support and unpatched device reports support the cyber standard, and the asset register doubles as the information asset register for systems.

### Gaps
- No NCSC "Cyber Assessment Framework-lite" for schools was found.
- Search found nothing on whether Police CyberAlarm will continue. The Sept 2026 RPA rules still require it.
- DfE states there is no mandatory statutory cyber-incident reporting duty for schools. This came from a search-result summary only ([Schools Week / PublicTechnology via search](https://www.publictechnology.net/2025/04/10/education-and-skills/dfe-alerted-to-more-than-50-school-ransomware-attacks-in-past-three-years/)) and was not verified.

## 9. Freedom of Information requests touching IT records

### Takeaway
Maintained schools and academy trusts (at trust board level) must answer FOI requests within 20 school days or 60 working days, whichever is shorter. IT records are "information held": asset counts, IT spend, contracts and supplier lists. Schools must also keep a list of destroyed records. A helpdesk with reporting, search/export, redaction and an FOI request ticket type with a school-day clock would help.

### Cited Findings
- **ICO advice for schools**:
  - "You must respond promptly, within 20 school days"
  - the outer limit is "60 working days"
  - state schools "must respond to requests for information from the public"
  - for academies, responsibility sits with the trust
  - schools should keep a publication scheme, track requests (the ICO offers a request-tracking template), and consider "providing an anonymised version" where personal data is involved
  - Source: [ICO: Advice for schools on the FOIA](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/learning-resources-and-training-videos/advice-for-schools-on-the-foia-and-responding-to-requests-for-information/)
- **ICO section 10 guidance**: the clock starts when the organisation receives the request, not when it reaches the FOI officer (search summary). — [ICO: time limits (s10)](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/time-limits-for-compliance-under-the-freedom-of-information-act-section-10/)
- **Destroyed-records list**: the FOIA requires "a list of records that have been destroyed and who authorised their destruction". — [DfE: Record keeping](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-protection-in-schools/record-keeping-and-management). IRMS adds that the school "should be able to provide evidence that the destruction of records took place as part of a routine records management process". — [IRMS 2019](https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/media/1219915/irms-toolkit-for-schools-2019.pdf)

### Inferences
- Add an FOI request ticket type with a 20-school-day / 60-working-day clock. This needs term dates in the SLA calendar, which EduHelpdesk does not yet have.
- Pre-built reports for common IT FOI asks: device counts by type and age, IT spend by year and supplier, contracts and renewal dates, support/ITAD/disposal suppliers.
- Export with redaction of personal names.
- The scheduled destruction register (section 2) provides the "routine process" evidence.

#### Cross-cutting capability shortlist (ranked by how many obligations each serves; inference)
1. **Retention and disposal engine.** Per-category purge or anonymise rules, legal hold, a destruction register with authoriser, and backup coverage noted. Serves: UK GDPR storage limitation, the DfE annual audit, IRMS, the FOIA destroyed-records list, and the ROPA retention column.
2. **Asset lifecycle evidence.** Covers:
   - fixed asset / inventory fields (cost, funding, capitalised flag, portable-and-attractive flag)
   - annual stock check with variance and write-off approval
   - loss/theft register with police reference and the £5,000 ATH aggregate
   - disposal with WEEE, transfer-note and data-destruction certificates
   - end-of-support dates
   - an off-site export

   Serves: ATH 2.7/5.19–5.24/6.10, SFVS Q8/Q27, the scheme inventory requirement, RPA claims and exclusions, the DfE device and cyber standards, NCSC sanitisation, and waste duty of care.
