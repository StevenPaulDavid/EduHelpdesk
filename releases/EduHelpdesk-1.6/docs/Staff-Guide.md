# Reporting an IT problem — a guide for staff

For teachers, teaching assistants, office staff and anyone else who needs to tell the IT team something is wrong.

> **Note for IT before you hand this out:** replace "your school's helpdesk address" below with the real URL, and check the categories and locations listed match the ones you have set up. Everything else applies as written.

---

## Where to go

Open your school's helpdesk address in a browser — ask IT if you are not sure what it is. It ends in `/Portal`.

You can reach it from any school computer on the network. You will land on the sign-in page.

---

## Signing in

- **Email** — your school email address.
- **Password** — set for you by the IT team. The first time you sign in with it, you'll be asked to choose your own password, which only you know. At least 8 characters; three random words together works well.
- **Keep me signed in on this device for 30 days** — tick this only on your own laptop. Leave it unticked on a shared or staffroom computer: you'll be signed out when the browser closes, or after two hours of not using the portal.

You can change your password at any time from **Change password** on the portal's home page.

If you have never signed in before, or you have forgotten your password, **ask IT directly**. There is no "forgot password" link and no reset email — a technician has to set it for you.

---

## Reporting a problem

1. Sign in, then choose **Report a problem**.

2. **What's the problem?**
   One short line describing it. This is the only thing you have to fill in.
   *For example: "Projector in D12 has no signal"*

3. **Tell us more (optional)**
   Anything that helps — what you already tried, when it started, the exact wording of any error message.

4. **What kind of problem is it?**
   Pick the closest match. The standard list is Hardware, Software, Account, Network, Classroom AV and Other.

5. **Where is it?**
   Pick the room or area. Leave it as "Not specified" if it does not apply.

6. **Attach files (optional)**
   Up to three files, each up to 10 MB: a photo of the error message, a screenshot, a document that won't open. Pictures, PDFs, Office documents and text files are accepted.

7. **Submit.**
   Your problem gets a ticket number, like #1042. Quote that number if you speak to IT about it.

### What makes a report easy to act on

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "Whiteboard broken" | "IWB pen in D12 not writing on the left third of the board. Started Monday. The spare pen does the same." |
| "Can't log in" | "Year 8 pupil can't log in on the trolley laptops in B6 — says 'account restricted'. Works fine on the desktop." |
| "Printer not working" | "Staffroom printer jams on every A3 job. A4 is fine. Been like it since Friday." |

The things worth including every time: **which room or device**, **what you expected to happen**, **what actually happened**, **when it started**, and **what you have already tried**.

A photo is often the quickest way to show what's wrong - attach it to the report, or to a message later.

## What happens next

**Nothing will email you.** The system does not send notifications of any kind — no confirmation, no update, no "we've closed it". To find out how your report is getting on, sign in and look: the portal's home page shows how many of your tickets have news, and **My tickets** marks each one with **New reply** until you open it.

The IT team decides how urgent it is. You do not set the priority yourself, though you can see it once the ticket is open.

Your ticket will be in one of four states:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Received, nobody has started on it yet |
| **In Progress** | Someone is working on it now |
| **On Hold** | Waiting on something — a part on order, a supplier, or an answer from you |
| **Closed** | Finished |

If a ticket is **On Hold**, it is worth checking whether it is waiting on *you* — open it and look at the updates.

---

## Checking on something you reported

Choose **My tickets**. You will see everything you have reported, with its current status, newest first.

Open any one of them to see:

- The status, priority, category and location.
- What you originally told us.
- **Files** — anything you attached, and anything the IT team has shared with you.
- **Updates** — messages from the IT team.
- A box to **add a message** of your own, with files if you like.

Use the message box to add anything new: it has got worse, it has fixed itself, you have found a workaround, or you are away next week.

**If a closed ticket's problem comes back** within two weeks, just send a message on it: the ticket reopens and the team see it. After that, the ticket shows **Report it again** instead, which starts a new report already filled in from the old one and linked to it, so the technician can see what was tried last time.

You only ever see **your own** tickets. Technicians also keep private working notes that do not appear here — if it is not in Updates, it was not meant for you, not because anything is being hidden.

---

## When not to use the portal

Because nothing alerts the IT team the moment you submit something, the portal is the wrong tool when you need help *now*. Go and find a technician, or phone the IT office, for:

- A whole class or room that is down **mid-lesson**
- Anything to do with **safeguarding or security**
- Problems with a room about to be used for an **exam, assembly or parents' evening**
- Anything **unsafe** — a burning smell, damaged cabling, a plug or charger that is hot

Report it in the portal afterwards as well, so there is a record of it.

## Common questions

**I can't sign in.**
Ask IT to set your password. Portal accounts are created by the IT team, so if you have never been given one, that is probably why.

**I've forgotten my password.**
Ask IT. There is no self-service reset.

**It says "Too many failed sign-ins".**
After five wrong passwords, the portal stops accepting that email for 15 minutes. Wait and try again, or ask IT to set you a new password, which unlocks it straight away.

**My ticket was closed but the problem is back.**
If it closed in the last two weeks, send a message on it - that reopens it, so the team see it again. After that, use **Report it again** on the old ticket.

**Can I send a photo?**
Yes - attach it when you report the problem, or with any message afterwards. Up to three files at a time, 10 MB each.

**Can I see what a colleague reported?**
No. You only see your own.

**Can I make something more urgent?**
Not directly — priority is set by the IT team. If something has become genuinely urgent, add a message saying what has changed, and for anything that cannot wait, speak to IT in person.

**I've fixed it myself / I don't need it any more.**
Open the ticket and add a message saying so, and the team will close it.

**Can I make it darker, or lighter?**
Yes, just for the device you're using: at the bottom of every portal page, **Appearance** offers Light, Dark, or Match device, which follows your computer's or phone's own setting. Press the one you've chosen again to go back to the school's standard look.

**What does the helpdesk keep about me?**
Your name, email, department and location, the problems you've reported with their updates, and any equipment lent to you. Ask the school's data protection lead if you want a copy: IT can produce one. When you leave, your portal account is switched off, and the school's retention policy decides how long your old reports are kept.

**Do I need a separate login for anything else?**
No. This login only works for the staff portal. The full system that technicians use is a separate login that ordinary staff accounts do not have.
