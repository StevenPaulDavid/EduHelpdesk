Statutory checks - the weekly fire alarm test, monthly water temperatures, emergency lighting - are logged as tickets automatically on their schedule. Each comes with a **checklist** to fill in.

![A checklist on a ticket](checklist.png)

## Filling in the checklist

Open the check's ticket. The checklist is under the description. For each line:

- **Pass or fail** lines - choose *Pass* or *Fail*.
- **Reading** lines (*reading with limits*) - type the number you measured, such as the water temperature. It shows what it should be (*50 °C or more*). A reading outside that counts as a **fail**.
- **Note** lines - write what was done. These are never required.

Add a note to any line if it helps, such as *Tap ran warm after 2 minutes*. Press **Save checklist** as you go; you don't have to do it all at once. Each line records who answered it and when.

## Closing the check

The ticket **can't be closed until every line is answered** - not from the ticket, and not in bulk from the ticket list. Once it is, **Mark as closed** as usual.

If anything failed, closing it creates **one follow-up job** called *Failed check: ...*, listing every failure and its reading, linked to the check. Fix the problems on that job. Closing the check again later doesn't create a second one.

Once a check is closed its answers are locked. To change one, reopen the ticket by changing its status.

## The record

Every check, with its readings, failures, who did it and whether it was on time, is in **Reports → Premises checks**, which can be printed for governors, insurers or an inspection.

Checks are set up by someone who can change settings. See [Setting up a premises check](set-up-premises-checks).
